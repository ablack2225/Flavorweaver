Red-Chile Three-Cheese Enchiladas

Homemade corn tortillas rolled around sharp cheddar, pepper jack, mozzarella, and finely diced onion, then baked beneath a silky guajillo-enriched red-chile gravy. The target is unmistakable chile-and-cheese enchilada flavor—not canned sauce, queso dip, or casserole.

Yield6 enchiladas
Total TimeAbout 50 minutes
DifficultyModerate
Best ServiceFresh from the oven
Three CheeseGuajilloHomemade TortillasTex-Mex InspiredFully Measured

Kitchen Execution Summary

Red-Chile Gravy

Toast and soak one guajillo, blend it with canned red enchilada sauce, broth, and garlic, then cook it through a light cumin-oregano roux until glossy and spoon-coating.

Cheese Filling

Sharp cheddar provides identity, pepper jack provides heat and melt, and mozzarella provides cohesion. Finely diced onion keeps the filling recognizably enchilada-style.

Tortillas

Thawed homemade corn tortillas receive only a quick pass through a thin film of oil so they become pliable without frying crisp.

Critical Control

Do not drown or overbake the enchiladas. Pull them as soon as the sauce bubbles and the cheese is fully melted.

Before Cooking / Advance Prep

Earlier in the day: Shred the three cheeses, dice the onion, and stem and seed the guajillo. Keep cheese refrigerated.

Do not do early: Do not oil-condition or fill the tortillas until the sauce is finished and the baking dish is ready.

Service Countdown

45 minutes before serving: Heat the oven to 375°F and begin the red-chile gravy.

25 minutes before serving: Spread sauce in the dish, condition the tortillas, and roll all six enchiladas.

18 minutes before serving: Bake until bubbling and fully melted.

At service: Rest 5 minutes, garnish lightly, and serve before the tortillas soften.

Full Enchilada Ingredient Roll-Up

Quick-glance ingredient check for all six enchiladas. Exact divisions are repeated in the prep bowls and cooking timeline.

Red-Chile Gravy

Dried guajillo, 10-ounce can red enchilada sauce, low-sodium broth, garlic, neutral oil or butter, flour, cumin, Mexican oregano, black pepper, optional sugar correction, and salt only after tasting.

Three-Cheese Filling

Sharp cheddar, pepper jack, low-moisture mozzarella, and very finely diced white, yellow, or sweet onion.

Tortillas

Six fully thawed homemade corn tortillas and 1–2 tablespoons neutral oil for a very thin skillet film.

Fresh Finish / Table

Optional onion or green onion, cilantro, jalapeño, sour cream, and lime wedges.

Measured Ingredients by Component

These sections are reference. The complete one-pass cooking flow appears below.

Elevated Red-Chile Gravy

1 dried guajillo chile, stemmed and seeded; 10 oz red enchilada sauce; 1/2 cup low-sodium chicken or beef broth, plus 1–3 Tbsp only if needed; 1 garlic clove; 1 Tbsp neutral oil or unsalted butter; 1 Tbsp all-purpose flour; 1/2 tsp ground cumin; 1/2 tsp Mexican oregano; 1/4 tsp black pepper; up to 1/4 tsp sugar only if harsh; fine salt only after tasting.

Three-Cheese Filling and Topping

1 cup / about 4 oz sharp cheddar; 3/4 cup / about 3 oz pepper jack; 1/2 cup / about 2 oz low-moisture mozzarella; 1/3 cup very finely diced onion. Mix all 2 1/4 cups cheese. Reserve 3/4 cup topping cheese; use 1 1/2 cups filling cheese, 1/4 cup per tortilla.

Tortillas and Fresh Finish

6 homemade corn tortillas, fully thawed; 1–2 Tbsp neutral oil. Optional finish: 2–3 Tbsp diced onion or sliced green onion, 2 Tbsp chopped cilantro, jalapeño, sour cream, and lime wedges.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Cheese: Shred all three cheeses, combine them, then physically remove and label 3/4 cup for topping before any tortillas are filled.

Onion: Measure 1/3 cup finely diced onion for the filling. Prepare any garnish onion separately so the filling amount is protected.

Broth: Measure 1/2 cup for the blender and keep a small additional amount nearby only for consistency correction.

Stage-Based Prep Bowls

Sauce Workflow

Bowl 1 — Blender Cup

1 softened guajillo, 10 oz red enchilada sauce, 1/2 cup low-sodium broth, and 1 peeled garlic clove.

Bowl 2 — Roux and Spices

1 Tbsp oil or butter, 1 Tbsp flour, 1/2 tsp cumin, 1/2 tsp Mexican oregano, and 1/4 tsp black pepper.

Bowl 3 — Adjustment

Extra broth, fine salt, and up to 1/4 tsp sugar only if the finished sauce requires correction.

Assembly Workflow

Bowl 1 — Filling

1 1/2 cups combined cheese plus 1/3 cup very finely diced onion.

Bowl 2 — Topping

Reserved 3/4 cup combined cheese, kept separate until all enchiladas are rolled and sauced.

Bowl 3 — Table Finish

Optional fresh onion, cilantro, jalapeño, sour cream, and lime wedges.

Do Not Forget

Toast the guajillo only until fragrant and flexible. Burnt guajillo will make the entire gravy bitter.

Reserve 3/4 cup cheese before filling the first tortilla.

Condition tortillas in only a thin film of oil until hot and pliable—not crisp.

Use enough gravy to coat and protect the tortillas, but do not drown the baking dish.

Stop baking as soon as the sauce bubbles and the cheese is fully melted.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Heat the oven and stage the filling. Heat the oven to 375°F. Shred 1 cup sharp cheddar, 3/4 cup pepper jack, and 1/2 cup low-moisture mozzarella. Combine the cheeses, reserve 3/4 cup for topping, and leave 1 1/2 cups for filling. Very finely dice 1/3 cup onion.
  2. Toast and soak the guajillo. Toast 1 stemmed and seeded guajillo in a dry skillet over medium for 10–15 seconds per side, only until fragrant. Cover with very hot water for 10 minutes, then drain.
  3. Blend the sauce foundation. Blend the softened guajillo with 10 oz red enchilada sauce, 1/2 cup low-sodium broth, and 1 peeled garlic clove for 45–60 seconds until smooth. Strain for the silkiest gravy.
  4. Build the red-chile gravy. Heat 1 Tbsp oil or butter over medium-low. Whisk in 1 Tbsp flour and cook 60–90 seconds without browning. Stir in 1/2 tsp cumin, 1/2 tsp Mexican oregano, and 1/4 tsp black pepper for 15 seconds. Slowly whisk in the blended sauce and simmer gently for 8–10 minutes. Add broth 1 Tbsp at a time only if too thick. Taste before adding salt or up to 1/4 tsp sugar.
  5. Prepare the baking dish. Spread 1/2 cup finished gravy across the bottom of an approximately 8-by-8-inch dish. Keep the remaining gravy warm over the lowest heat.
  6. Condition the tortillas. Heat a skillet over medium with only enough of the 1–2 Tbsp neutral oil to leave a thin sheen. Warm each tortilla for 5–8 seconds per side until supple but not crisp. Stack under a clean towel.
  7. Fill and roll. Place 1/4 cup filling cheese and about 2 1/2 tsp finely diced onion in each warm tortilla. Roll gently and arrange seam-side down in the prepared dish.
  8. Sauce and top. Spoon the remaining gravy over the enchiladas, concentrating it over the seams while leaving some outer edges visible. Scatter the reserved 3/4 cup topping cheese over the center.
  9. Bake and rest. Bake uncovered at 375°F for 15–18 minutes, until bubbling and fully melted. Broil only 30–60 seconds if browned spots are desired. Rest 5 minutes.
  10. Serve. Finish lightly with onion, cilantro, jalapeño, or sour cream. Serve lime wedges alongside so each plate can be brightened without flattening the red-chile gravy.

Final Taste Target

The first bite should lead with toasted red chile and sharp cheddar, followed by creamy pepper jack, restrained mozzarella stretch, and a clean onion bite. The sauce should taste deeper and silkier than it did from the can while remaining clearly red-chile gravy. The tortillas should be tender and sauced but still structured enough to serve cleanly.

Chef's Notes

Focused architecture: Velveeta Queso Blanco, Hot Rotel, mole, and chorizo are intentionally excluded. They would blur the identity of this cheese-enchilada recipe.

Cheese roles: Cheddar supplies flavor, pepper jack supplies creamy heat, and mozzarella supplies cohesion. The filling is deliberately not mozzarella-heavy.

First test: Record the homemade tortilla diameter, exact sauce usage, cheese balance, and whether Don wants more onion or chile heat before promoting the recipe.
