Chipotle-Guajillo Lime Brisket with Blackened Fresh-Plum Salsa

Thin brisket is marinated in a cooked, strained guajillo-chipotle sauce, seared in small batches, and finished in a clean smoky lime glaze. A required large-batch salsa layers skillet-blackened plums, tomatoes, onion, garlic, and chiles with fresh plums, cilantro, lime, and a guajillo-honey vinegar reduction.

Prep1 hr 15 min
CookAbout 35 min
Serves4-6
Marinate30-90 min

Kitchen Execution Summary

Cooked Chile Foundation

Guajillos are toasted and rehydrated, then blended with charred onion and garlic, chipotle, orange, lime zest, soy, honey, spices, and MSG. The sauce is strained and cooked before it touches the beef.

Short Brisket Marinade

Thin brisket receives only 30-90 minutes in the lime-finished marinade so the meat gains flavor without prolonged acid exposure.

High-Heat Sear

The brisket cooks in 3-4 small batches so it develops dark edges rather than steaming. Clean reserved sauce becomes the final glossy pan glaze.

Required Plum Salsa

Half the fruit and vegetables are deeply blackened in a dry skillet; fresh plums, cilantro, green onion, raw jalapeño, lime, and olive oil preserve brightness and texture.

Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

Salsa head start — The blackened fresh-plum salsa may be made several hours before service. Stir and retaste after chilling.

Brisket slicing — Slice the brisket 1/8- to 1/4-inch thick strictly across the grain before marinating.

Clean sauce separation — Reserve 1/2 cup cooked master chile sauce before the remaining sauce touches raw meat.

Service Countdown

2 hours before serving: make the guajillo-honey reduction, blacken the salsa produce, process the salsa, and refrigerate.

90 minutes before serving: toast and rehydrate brisket guajillos, char sauce aromatics, blend, strain, cook, and cool the master sauce.

30-90 minutes before serving: marinate the brisket.

20 minutes before serving: drain the brisket, preheat the skillet, stage the clean sauce, lime, salsa, and optional vegetables.

At service: sear in batches, reduce the clean glaze, toss briefly, rest 5-10 minutes, and serve with lightly drained salsa for bread or full salsa juices for bowls.

Full Recipe Ingredient Roll-Up

Quick-glance check for the entire brisket and salsa build.

Master Chile Sauce

Dried guajillos, sweet onion, garlic, chipotle in adobo, orange juice, soaking liquid or broth, lime zest, soy sauce, honey, cumin, coriander, Mexican oregano, smoked paprika, black pepper, MSG, olive oil.

Brisket and Glaze

Thin-sliced brisket, cooked chile sauce, lime juice, fine salt, tallow or oil, clean reserved sauce, water or broth.

Blackened Plum Salsa

Fresh plums, Roma tomatoes, onion, jalapeño, fresh hot red Mexican chile, garlic, green onions, cilantro, lime, olive oil, salt, MSG.

Guajillo-Honey Reduction

Dried guajillo, unseasoned rice vinegar, white vinegar, and honey.

Measured Ingredients by Component

Use these sections for measuring; follow the stage-based bowls and full timeline for execution.

Master Chipotle-Guajillo Sauce

3 dried guajillos, stemmed and seeded; 1/3 large sweet onion, about 1 cup wedges; 5 garlic cloves in skins; 1 chipotle in adobo plus 1 Tbsp adobo sauce; 1/3 cup orange juice; 1/4 cup soaking liquid or low-sodium broth; zest of 1 lime; 2 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 1 Tbsp honey; 1 tsp each ground cumin, ground coriander, and Mexican oregano; 1/2 tsp each smoked paprika, black pepper, and MSG; 1 Tbsp olive oil.

Brisket Marinade, Sear, and Glaze

1 1/2 lb brisket, sliced 1/8- to 1/4-inch thick across grain; 3/4 cup cooled master sauce; 1 Tbsp lime juice; 1/2 tsp fine salt; 1-2 Tbsp tallow or neutral oil; 1/2 cup clean reserved sauce; 2-4 Tbsp water or low-sodium broth; 1-2 Tbsp fresh lime juice for finishing.

Guajillo-Honey Vinegar Reduction

1 dried guajillo, stemmed, seeded, lightly toasted, and torn; 3 Tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar; 1 Tbsp white vinegar; 1 Tbsp plus 1 tsp honey.

Large-Batch Blackened Fresh-Plum Salsa

1 1/2 lb ripe but firm plums, divided half charred and half fresh; 2 Roma tomatoes, halved and cored only; 1/2 medium red or sweet onion; 1 whole jalapeño for charring plus up to 1/2 raw jalapeño; 1/2-1 fresh hot red Mexican chile; 3 skin-on garlic cloves; 2 green onions; 1/2 cup packed cilantro; 2-3 Tbsp lime juice; 2 tsp olive oil; 3/4-1 tsp fine salt; optional 1/4 tsp MSG; one batch guajillo-honey reduction.

Optional Charred Onion and Pepper

1 medium sweet onion, cut into 1/2-inch wedges; 1 large bell pepper, broad strips; 1 tsp tallow or oil; pinch fine salt.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Guajillos: use 3 for the brisket master sauce and 1 for the salsa reduction.

Lime: zest one lime into the master sauce; use juice only in the short marinade, salsa finish, and final clean glaze.

Garlic and onion: char separate measured portions for the chile sauce and salsa. Keep green onions fresh for the salsa.

Chile heat: chipotle supplies smoky depth to the brisket; jalapeño and the fresh red chile supply layered salsa heat. Add the unidentified red chile gradually.

Plums: char half for dark depth and keep half fresh so the salsa remains juicy and bright.

Stage-Based Prep Bowls

Master Sauce and Brisket

Tray 1 — Guajillos

3 stemmed and seeded dried guajillos for toasting and rehydrating.

Bowl 2 — Blender Build

Charred onion and garlic, rehydrated guajillos, chipotle and adobo, orange juice, soaking liquid, lime zest, soy, honey, cumin, coriander, oregano, smoked paprika, black pepper, MSG, olive oil.

Bowl 3 — Clean Finish

1/2 cup reserved master sauce, 2-4 Tbsp water or broth, and 1-2 Tbsp lime juice held separately.

Salsa

Pan 1 — Vinegar Reduction

1 toasted guajillo, 3 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp white vinegar, 1 Tbsp plus 1 tsp honey.

Tray 2 — Blacken

Half the plums, 2 Roma tomatoes, 1/2 onion, 1 jalapeño, 1/2-1 red chile, and 3 skin-on garlic cloves.

Bowl 3 — Fresh Finish

Remaining plums, up to 1/2 raw jalapeño, 2 green onions, 1/2 cup cilantro, 2 Tbsp lime juice, 2 tsp olive oil, 3/4 tsp salt, optional 1/4 tsp MSG.

Do Not Forget

Toast guajillos only until fragrant; burned dried chile creates lasting bitterness.

Reserve clean master sauce before raw brisket enters the workflow.

Marinate thin brisket only 30-90 minutes after lime juice is added.

Sear in 3-4 uncrowded batches and separate tangled slices before cooking.

Use short food-processor pulses so the salsa remains textured.

Keep tomato seeds and gel; drain only the finished salsa portion used inside bread.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Make the salsa reduction. Toast 1 guajillo for 10-15 seconds per side. Simmer it with 3 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp white vinegar, and 1 Tbsp plus 1 tsp honey for 4-6 minutes, reducing by roughly half. Steep 5 minutes, strain, and cool.
  2. Blacken the salsa produce. In a very hot dry skillet, work in batches to blacken half the plums, both Roma tomatoes, 1/2 onion, 1 jalapeño, 1/2 red chile to begin, and 3 skin-on garlic cloves. Remove each ingredient when deeply blistered but not collapsed. Cool and peel the garlic.
  3. Process the blackened foundation. Pulse the blackened produce with about two-thirds of the reduction, 3/4 tsp salt, and optional 1/4 tsp MSG until finely chopped and cohesive.
  4. Add fresh salsa brightness. Add the remaining fresh plums, up to 1/2 raw jalapeño, 2 green onions, 1/2 cup cilantro, 2 Tbsp lime juice, and 2 tsp olive oil. Pulse briefly until textured. Rest 15-20 minutes and adjust salt, lime, heat, and reduction.
  5. Toast and soak the brisket guajillos. Toast 3 guajillos for 10-15 seconds per side, cover with hot water, and soak 15 minutes. Reserve 1/4 cup strained liquid.
  6. Char the sauce aromatics. Blacken 1 cup onion wedges and 5 skin-on garlic cloves. Peel the garlic.
  7. Blend and cook the master sauce. Blend the soaked guajillos and charred aromatics with the chipotle and adobo, orange juice, soaking liquid, lime zest, soy, honey, cumin, coriander, oregano, smoked paprika, black pepper, MSG, and olive oil. Strain and simmer 6-8 minutes until lightly thickened. Cool.
  8. Divide and marinate. Reserve 1/2 cup clean sauce. Mix 3/4 cup sauce, 1 Tbsp lime juice, and 1/2 tsp salt with 1 1/2 lb thin brisket. Refrigerate 30-90 minutes.
  9. Drain and stage. Let excess marinade drip away, pat the surfaces lightly, discard raw marinade, and separate the slices.
  10. Char optional vegetables. Heat the skillet to approximately 375-400°F. Char onion wedges and bell-pepper strips with 1 tsp tallow or oil; season lightly and remove.
  11. Sear the brisket. Add a thin film of tallow or oil and cook in 3-4 uncrowded batches, about 1-3 minutes each depending on thickness, until deeply browned and just cooked through.
  12. Build the clean glaze. Lower heat to medium. Add 1/2 cup clean sauce and 2 Tbsp water or broth, scrape up the fond, and simmer until glossy. Add more liquid only if needed. Turn off heat and stir in 1 Tbsp lime juice.
  13. Glaze briefly. Return brisket and juices to the pan for only 30-60 seconds. Fold in charred vegetables if using. Taste with salsa before making final salt or lime adjustments.
  14. Rest and serve. Rest 5-10 minutes. Serve in toasted sub rolls, wraps, rice bowls, tacos, or lettuce cups with the blackened fresh-plum salsa.

Troubleshooting / Rescue Notes

Brisket will not brown: dry it more thoroughly, raise the heat, and use smaller batches.

Brisket is chewy: confirm the grain direction, chop into shorter pieces, and warm briefly in the glaze instead of continuing to fry.

Chile sauce is bitter: soften mild bitterness with orange, broth, and honey; remake if a scorched-chile flavor dominates.

Salsa is watery: rest it, then drain only the portion used for sandwiches.

Salsa became puree: fold in hand-chopped fresh plum, onion, cilantro, and lime.

Salsa tastes flat: adjust salt before adding more lime.

Final Taste Target

The brisket should taste dark, smoky, citrus-aromatic, savory, and chile-forward, with browned edges and a clean glossy finish rather than a heavy wet sauce. The salsa should taste fresh first—juicy plum, lime, cilantro, and clean chile heat—followed by deep blackened tomato, onion, garlic, and fruit. Together, the salsa should lift the brisket rather than mirror its spice profile.

Chef's Notes

Lime zest carries citrus aroma in the master sauce without tightening the thin brisket. Most lime juice remains in the short marinade, final glaze, and salsa where it stays vivid.

No cumin or smoked paprika belongs in the salsa. Those notes are already present in the brisket; the salsa's role is blackened produce, fresh fruit, acid, herbs, and clean chile heat.

Keep tomato seeds and gel. They carry flavor, and moisture is better controlled after processing.
