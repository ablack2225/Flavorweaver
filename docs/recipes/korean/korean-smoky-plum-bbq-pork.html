Korean Smoky-Plum BBQ Pork Hoagies

Two soft homemade hoagie rolls filled with overnight-marinated pork butt that is hard-seared, gently braised until pull-apart tender, rested in its juices, coarse-pulled, and lacquered with a smoky fresh-plum gochujang barbecue glaze. Spicy mayo provides creamy heat while cold kosher dill pickles and paper-thin purple onion provide clean acidity and bite without turning the sandwich into a dressed slaw.

Kitchen Execution Summary

Full Pork Batch

The already-marinated 2 lb pork batch is cooked completely, but only enough for two hoagies is lacquered tonight.

Pull-Apart Texture

Hard-sear, cover-braise at 315°F, rest in liquid, coarse-pull, and restore reduced juices before dividing.

Two-Plum Glaze

Broiled plums add roasted depth while uncharred plums preserve fresh acidity and prevent a jammy sauce.

Clean Cold Contrast

Dry kosher dill slices and raw paper-thin purple onion replace the cabbage-based dressed crunch.

Two-Hoagie Mayo

A smaller spicy gochujang mayo batch seasons both toasted faces without overwhelming the pork.

Reserved Pork

Extra pork is packed with reduced braising liquid before plum lacquering so it stays moist and versatile.

Advance Prep

Already complete: marinate the 2 lb pork butt for 8-24 hours.

Tonight: thaw and proof only 2 frozen shaped hoagie rolls.

Make ahead: plum glaze and spicy mayo may be prepared up to 2 days ahead.

Close to service: slice the purple onion paper-thin and pat the sliced kosher dills very dry.

Start Here: Complete Kitchen Execution Order

1. Start Two Rolls

Thaw and proof only two shaped hoagie rolls under lightly oiled plastic.

2. Sear and Braise

Drain and dry the overnight-marinated pork, hard-sear in batches, then cover-braise at 315°F.

3. Build Sauce and Mayo

Make the two-plum barbecue glaze and the smaller two-hoagie spicy mayo during the braise.

4. Cook to Pull-Apart

Continue until a fork enters with almost no resistance and twisting separates the meat.

5. Restore and Divide

Rest in liquid, coarse-pull, restore juices, then reserve the extra pork before lacquering.

6. Lacquer and Build Two

Broil only 10-12 oz pork, toast the two rolls, and finish with dry dill and paper-thin onion.

Non-Negotiable Texture Standard

Cook until pull-apart: expect roughly 2½-3¼ hours at 315°F after searing, but tenderness—not the clock—decides.

Rest in liquid: leave the tender pork covered in its braising liquid 20-30 minutes.

Coarse-pull: make substantial short shreds and tender pieces, not dry hairlike strands.

Restore moisture first: fold reduced braising liquid into all the meat before dividing.

Reserve before lacquer: only the two-hoagie portion receives the sugar-rich plum glaze and broiler finish.

Measured Ingredients by Component

Pork and Overnight Marinade

2 lb pork butt, cut into 1½- to 2-inch chunks; 2 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 2 Tbsp gochujang; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar; 1 Tbsp honey; ⅓ cup grated sweet onion; 6 grated garlic cloves; 1 Tbsp grated ginger; whites/light greens from 2 green onions; 1 tsp gochugaru; ½ tsp black pepper; ½ tsp MSG; 1 tsp toasted sesame oil.

Savory Braise

1 Tbsp oil; 1 cup sliced sweet onion; ¾ cup lower-sodium chicken broth; ½ cup water; 1 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 1 Tbsp gochujang; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar.

Charred Fresh-Plum BBQ Glaze

1½ lb fresh plums, skins on, halved and pitted; 2 Tbsp gochujang; 3 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; ¼ cup rice vinegar; 3 Tbsp honey; up to 1 Tbsp brown sugar; 1 Tbsp tomato paste; 1 Tbsp grated ginger; 4 grated garlic cloves; 1 tsp smoked paprika; 1 tsp gochugaru; ½ cup water; 1 tsp toasted sesame oil off heat.

Spicy Gochujang Mayo — Two Hoagies

¼ cup mayonnaise; 2 tsp gochujang; 1 tsp lime juice; ½ tsp rice vinegar; ½ tsp honey; small pinch garlic powder; ⅛ tsp toasted sesame oil; optional pinch gochugaru.

Kosher Dill and Purple Onion Finish

6-10 thin kosher dill pickle slices, or 2 spears sliced thinly; ¼ small purple onion, sliced into nearly transparent half-moons or wispy layers; optional 1 sliced green onion and ½ tsp toasted sesame seeds.

Pat pickles very dry. Keep the onion raw and undressed. Use a 5-minute ice-water rinse only if it is unusually harsh, then drain and dry thoroughly.

Hoagie Rolls, Service Pork, and Reserve

2 frozen shaped unproofed hoagie rolls, about 135-150 g each; 1 Tbsp melted butter; 10-12 oz finished pork for tonight; remaining pork reserved with reduced braising liquid; clean plum sauce packed separately.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Gochujang: 2 Tbsp marinade; 1 Tbsp braise; 2 Tbsp plum glaze; 2 tsp mayo.

Rice vinegar: 1 Tbsp marinade; 1 Tbsp braise; ¼ cup glaze; ½ tsp mayo; extra for final glaze balance.

Pork: 10-12 oz for tonight's two hoagies; reserve the remainder before lacquering.

Plum glaze: keep clean sauce separate from reduced pork liquid and raw marinade.

Pickles and onion: slice close to service, keep dry, and do not dress.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Start 2 frozen rolls. Place them on parchment, cover with lightly oiled plastic, and thaw/proof until visibly puffy and a fingertip dent returns slowly, usually 3-5 hours. Refrigerate covered up to about 1 hour if ready early.
  2. Temper and dry the pork. Remove marinated pork 30-40 minutes before searing. Drain, lightly pat dry, discard raw marinade, and heat oven to 315°F.
  3. Sear in batches. Film a Dutch oven with oil and brown pork deeply in 2-3 uncrowded batches without cooking the centers through.
  4. Build the braise. Cook sliced onion in the rendered fat about 5 minutes. Add broth, water, soy, gochujang, and vinegar, scraping up all fond.
  5. Braise for genuine tenderness. Return pork and juices. Liquid should reach one-third to halfway up the chunks. Cover tightly and braise at 315°F. Begin checking around 2 hours 15 minutes, then every 20 minutes. Pork is ready when a fork enters with almost no resistance and twisting separates the meat, commonly around 198-205°F.
  6. Char half the plums. Broil half the pitted plums cut-side up 6-10 minutes until blistered with dark caramelized spots.
  7. Make the plum glaze. Simmer all plums with gochujang, soy, vinegar, honey, optional brown sugar, tomato paste, ginger, garlic, smoked paprika, gochugaru, and water for 20-30 minutes.
  8. Blend, strain, and reduce. Blend smooth, strain through fine mesh, and reduce gently to about 1¾-2 cups. Remove from heat and stir in sesame oil. Correct jamminess with vinegar 1 tsp at a time.
  9. Make the two-hoagie mayo. Whisk all mayo ingredients smooth and chill at least 30 minutes.
  10. Rest the pork. When tender, remove the Dutch oven from the oven but leave pork tightly covered in its liquid for 20-30 minutes. Raise oven to 350°F.
  11. Bake the 2 proofed rolls. Bake 18-22 minutes until golden and 190-195°F internally. Brush warm tops lightly with butter and cool at least 20 minutes.
  12. Coarse-pull the pork. Lift pork to a wide bowl or tray and break into substantial short shreds and tender chunks.
  13. Reduce the pork liquid. Strain, skim excess fat, and boil to about ⅓ cup.
  14. Restore moisture to all pork. Fold about ¼ cup reduced braising liquid into the full coarse-pulled batch.
  15. Divide before lacquering. Measure 10-12 oz pork for tonight. Package the remaining pork with 1-2 Tbsp reduced liquid per portion. Refrigerate up to 3 days or freeze; store clean plum sauce separately.
  16. Build tonight's lacquer. Fold about ⅓ cup clean plum glaze into the service pork. Mix another 2 Tbsp glaze with 1 Tbsp reduced pork liquid for the broiler finish.
  17. Lacquer and char. Spread the service pork loosely on a foil-lined pan. Broil with two thin coats for a total of about 3-7 minutes, stopping when sticky dark edges appear while the meat remains juicy.
  18. Prepare the cold finish. Slice kosher dills thinly and pat very dry. Slice ¼ small purple onion into nearly transparent layers. Keep both raw and undressed.
  19. Butter-toast the rolls. Split without cutting through the hinge, brush cut faces lightly with butter, and toast cut-side down until golden.
  20. Build two hoagies. Spread mayo on both toasted faces. Add 5-6 oz lacquered pork per roll, no more than 1-2 tsp extra clean glaze only if needed, 3-5 dry dill slices, and a modest nest of paper-thin purple onion. Finish with optional scallion and sesame. Serve with the separately developed sides.

Service Countdown

5 hours before: begin covered thaw/proof of 2 rolls and temper pork.

4½ hours before: sear pork, build braise, and place covered in the 315°F oven.

During braise: make plum glaze and spicy mayo.

40 minutes before: rest tender pork covered in liquid; raise oven to 350°F.

35 minutes before: bake the 2 proofed rolls.

20 minutes before: coarse-pull pork, reduce liquid, restore moisture, and divide the batch.

10 minutes before: lacquer 10-12 oz pork, slice dry dills and paper-thin onion, and toast rolls.

Service: mayo → pork → optional clean glaze → kosher dill → purple onion → optional scallion and sesame.

Troubleshooting

Pork still tough: re-cover and continue braising in 20-minute increments.

Pork dry: fold in warm reduced braising liquid 1 Tbsp at a time before adding glaze.

Glaze jammy: add rice vinegar 1 tsp at a time, then a little water or soy if needed.

Pickles wet the bread: pat every slice dry and place them above the pork.

Onion overwhelms: use fewer paper-thin layers or rinse 5 minutes in ice water, then dry thoroughly.

Sandwich too sweet: add another dry dill slice or a few onion wisps; do not add more plum glaze.

Pairs Well With

The two final sides are intentionally not locked yet. They will be designed around the finished sandwich rather than added as generic filler.

Side slot 1: a distinct crisp, roasted, fried, or deeply savory element.

Side slot 2: a contrasting fresh, creamy, bright, or vegetable-forward element with its own flavor identity.

Chef's Note

The sandwich no longer uses a dressed vegetable crunch. That layer duplicated the spicy-sweet complexity already present in the plum barbecue sauce and gochujang mayo.

The cleaner finish is stronger: cold kosher dill supplies salt, garlic-dill acidity, and snap; raw paper-thin purple onion supplies fresh bite. The defining contrast is hot juicy-charred pork + cool sharp pickles + crisp raw onion + creamy spicy mayo + warm butter-toasted bread.

The full pork batch remains intentional because it was already marinated. Reserve the extra pork after restoring it with braising liquid but before adding the sugar-rich plum lacquer.
