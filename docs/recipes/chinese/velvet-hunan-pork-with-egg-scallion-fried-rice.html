Velvet Hunan Pork with Egg-Scallion Fried Rice

Melt-in-your-mouth velveted pork butt tossed with blistered green and red jalapeños, Thai chile, garlic, ginger, chili crisp, and a concentrated savory glaze, served with clean egg-scallion fried rice made from chicken-broth rice.

TotalAbout 1 hr 45 min
Serves2 generous or 3 moderate

Kitchen Execution Summary

Cook the Rice First

Chicken-broth basmati is cooked, spread thin immediately, cooled, then fried before the pork. Keep the finished fried rice loosely covered so it stays dry and separate.

Protect the Pork

Pork butt is sliced 1/8 inch across the grain and fully velveted with water, oyster sauce, soy, baking soda, cornstarch, and oil. Precook only until mostly opaque.

Blister the Peppers Dry

Green and red jalapeños are halved lengthwise, bias-cut into 1-inch pieces, and dry-blistered before the pork so they build char without steaming.

Finish Fast

Garlic, ginger, Thai chile, pork, peppers, and measured sauce reunite only at the end. The final 45 to 60 seconds create the glaze; do not keep cooking once the pork is tender and glossy.

Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

Rice — Cook 1 cup basmati rice with 1 3/4 cups chicken broth. Spread it thin as soon as it finishes, let visible steam escape, then refrigerate until cold and surface-dry.

Pork — Keep sliced pork refrigerated until the velvet window. Apply the measured velvet 30 to 40 minutes before cooking the pork; keep it refrigerated while it rests.

Skillet order — Fried rice cooks first. Hunan pork cooks last. The pork should go straight from the final skillet toss to the table.

Service Countdown

90 minutes before serving: cook the chicken-broth rice and spread it thin immediately.

45 minutes before serving: massage the measured velvet into the pork and refrigerate 30 to 40 minutes.

30 minutes before serving: cut peppers, garlic, ginger, onion, and scallions exactly as directed; beat eggs; mix both seasoning bowls.

18 minutes before serving: cook the egg-scallion fried rice and transfer it to a warm bowl.

9 minutes before serving: dry-blister the jalapeños, then precook the velveted pork in uncrowded batches.

3 minutes before serving: bloom aromatics, return peppers and pork, add the finishing sauce, and glaze.

At service: remove the pork from the skillet as soon as the thickest representative slices reach 145°F and the sauce clings with no puddle underneath.

Full Plate Ingredient Roll-Up

Quick-glance ingredient check for the complete meal. Detailed measurements and division points are handled below.

Velvet Pork

Pork butt, cold water, Shaoxing wine or chicken broth, oyster sauce, soy sauce, baking soda, cornstarch, neutral oil.

Hunan Pepper Glaze

Green jalapeños, red jalapeños, Thai chile, garlic, ginger, chili crisp, chili garlic sauce, soy, oyster sauce, dark soy or regular soy, rice vinegar, sugar, MSG, chicken broth.

Egg-Scallion Fried Rice

Basmati rice, chicken broth, eggs, neutral oil, onion, garlic, green onions, soy, oyster sauce, MSG, sesame oil.

Table Finish

Dark-green scallion tops in the fried rice; extra chili crisp at the table only if more heat is wanted.

Measured Ingredients by Component

These sections are reference only. The complete cook moves in one direction in the timeline below.

Chicken-Broth Rice Base

1 cup basmati rice, rinsed until mostly clear; 1 3/4 cups chicken broth.

Pork and Velvet

1 lb pork butt / pork shoulder, sliced 1/8 inch thick across the grain, pieces about 1 1/2 to 2 inches long where practical; 2 Tbsp cold water; 2 tsp Shaoxing wine or 2 tsp chicken broth; 2 tsp oyster sauce; 1 tsp regular/light soy sauce; 1/2 tsp baking soda; 2 tsp cornstarch; 2 tsp neutral oil.

Fresh Chiles and Aromatics

4 green jalapeños, stemmed, halved lengthwise, seeds/ribs retained, each half bias-cut into 1-inch pieces; 2 red jalapeños, cut the same way; 1 Thai chile, sliced into 1/8-inch rounds; 6 large garlic cloves, smashed, peeled, rough-chopped into 1/8- to 1/4-inch pieces; 1 Tbsp finely minced fresh ginger; 1 Tbsp neutral oil, plus 1 additional Tbsp only if needed for the second pork batch.

Hunan Finishing Sauce

1 Tbsp regular/light soy sauce; 2 tsp oyster sauce; 1 tsp dark soy sauce, or 1 tsp additional regular soy; 1 Tbsp chili crisp with oil and solids; 2 tsp chili garlic sauce; 1 tsp rice vinegar; 1/2 tsp sugar; 1/4 tsp MSG; 2 Tbsp chicken broth.

Egg-Scallion Fried Rice

All cooled rice from the 1-cup dry batch, about 3 cups cooked rice; 2 large eggs, beaten; 2 Tbsp neutral oil, divided; 1/4 cup white or sweet onion, 1/4-inch dice; 3 garlic cloves, finely minced; 3 green onions, sliced 1/8 inch thick with whites/light greens separated from dark-green tops; 1 Tbsp regular/light soy sauce; 1 tsp oyster sauce; 1 Tbsp chicken broth; 1/4 tsp MSG; 1 tsp sesame oil.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Chicken broth: 1 3/4 cups for rice; 2 tsp for the pork velvet only if Shaoxing is absent; 2 Tbsp for Hunan sauce; 1 Tbsp for fried-rice seasoning.

Garlic: 6 cloves rough-chopped for the pork; 3 cloves finely minced for fried rice. Keep the cuts separate.

Soy sauce: 1 tsp for velvet; 1 Tbsp for pork sauce; 1 Tbsp for fried rice; plus 1 tsp only if replacing dark soy.

Oyster sauce: 2 tsp for velvet; 2 tsp for pork sauce; 1 tsp for fried rice.

Green onions: slice 3 total; whites/light greens cook in fried rice, dark-green tops finish it.

Stage-Based Prep Bowls

Egg-Scallion Fried Rice Bowls

Bowl R1 — Eggs

2 beaten eggs.

Bowl R2 — Aromatics

1/4 cup 1/4-inch diced onion plus white/light-green portions from 3 sliced green onions.

Bowl R3 — Garlic

3 finely minced garlic cloves.

Bowl R4 — Rice Seasoning

1 Tbsp soy + 1 tsp oyster sauce + 1 Tbsp chicken broth + 1/4 tsp MSG.

Bowl R5 — Finish

Dark-green tops from 3 green onions plus 1 tsp sesame oil.

Velvet Hunan Pork Bowls

Bowl P1 — Velveted Pork

1 lb thin pork with its complete measured velvet; keep refrigerated until the pork stage.

Bowl P2 — Blister Peppers

4 green + 2 red jalapeños, halved lengthwise and bias-cut into 1-inch pieces.

Bowl P3 — Aromatics

6 rough-chopped garlic cloves + 1 Tbsp minced ginger + 1 Thai chile in 1/8-inch rounds.

Bowl P4 — Hunan Sauce

1 Tbsp soy + 2 tsp oyster sauce + 1 tsp dark soy/soy + 1 Tbsp chili crisp + 2 tsp chili garlic sauce + 1 tsp rice vinegar + 1/2 tsp sugar + 1/4 tsp MSG + 2 Tbsp broth.

Bowl P5 — Cooking Oil

1 Tbsp neutral oil, plus 1 Tbsp only if needed for the second pork batch.

Do Not Forget

Rice: get it out of the cooker and spread it thin immediately. Trapped steam makes soft fried rice.

Pork: use exactly 1/2 tsp baking soda per 1 lb pork; do not rinse after velveting.

Cut: pork is 1/8 inch across the grain. Jalapeños are stemmed, halved lengthwise, then bias-cut into 1-inch pieces — not rings.

Peppers: blister them dry with no oil and no water.

Pork batches: use two batches whenever a true single layer is not possible.

Precook: remove pork while mostly opaque and slightly underdone; do not brown hard.

Finish: the sauce is a glaze, not gravy. The last minute is the dish; stay at the skillet and remove the pork immediately when finished.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Cook and dry the rice first. Rinse 1 cup basmati rice until the water runs mostly clear and cook with 1 3/4 cups chicken broth. As soon as the cooker finishes, spread the rice in a thin loose layer on a sheet pan, platter, or wide baking dish. Let visible steam escape, then refrigerate until the grains are cold, dry on the surface, and easy to separate.
  2. Velvet the pork 30 to 40 minutes before its cook. Use 1 lb pork butt sliced 1/8 inch across the grain. Massage in 2 Tbsp cold water, 2 tsp Shaoxing wine or broth, 2 tsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp soy sauce, and 1/2 tsp baking soda for 60 to 90 seconds until the liquid is mostly absorbed. Add 2 tsp cornstarch, mix thoroughly, then add 2 tsp neutral oil last. Cover and refrigerate 30 to 40 minutes.
  3. Cut every vegetable before heat. Stem 4 green and 2 red jalapeños, halve lengthwise, retain ribs and seeds, and cut each half on a 45° bias into 1-inch pieces. Slice 1 Thai chile into 1/8-inch rounds. Rough-chop 6 smashed garlic cloves into 1/8- to 1/4-inch pieces and mince 1 Tbsp ginger. For fried rice, dice 1/4 cup onion into 1/4-inch pieces, mince 3 garlic cloves, and slice 3 green onions 1/8 inch thick, separating pale pieces from dark-green tops.
  4. Mix both seasoning bowls. Bowl R4: 1 Tbsp soy, 1 tsp oyster sauce, 1 Tbsp chicken broth, 1/4 tsp MSG. Bowl P4: 1 Tbsp soy, 2 tsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp dark soy or regular soy, 1 Tbsp chili crisp, 2 tsp chili garlic sauce, 1 tsp rice vinegar, 1/2 tsp sugar, 1/4 tsp MSG, 2 Tbsp chicken broth.
  5. Cook the fried rice first at 325°F. Preheat the electric skillet to 325°F. Add 1 tsp neutral oil and softly scramble 2 beaten eggs for 30 to 45 seconds; remove. Add 2 tsp more oil, then cook 1/4 cup diced onion and the pale pieces of 3 green onions for 60 to 90 seconds. Add 3 minced garlic cloves for only 15 to 20 seconds.
  6. Fry the cold rice until separate. Add the about 3 cups cold chicken-broth rice. Use the remaining neutral oil only as needed, never exceeding 2 Tbsp total rice oil. Break clumps, spread the rice for 45 seconds, toss, spread again for 45 to 60 seconds, then pour Bowl R4 around the hot rice. Toss 45 seconds. Return the eggs, add the dark-green scallion tops and 1 tsp sesame oil, toss 20 seconds, taste, and move the rice to a warm bowl. Cover loosely.
  7. Wipe the skillet and blister the peppers dry. Return the skillet to 325°F. Add all jalapeño pieces with no oil and no water. Spread them into one layer, leave 45 to 60 seconds, toss, then press and turn as needed for 3 to 5 minutes total until softened slightly with dark blistered patches. Remove.
  8. Velvet-cook the pork without finishing it. Add 1 Tbsp neutral oil at 325°F; reduce to 300°F if the coating colors immediately. Add pork only in a true single layer. Let it set 25 to 30 seconds, then turn and separate. Remove after about 60 to 90 seconds total, when mostly opaque but still slightly underdone in the centers. Repeat with the second batch, adding the reserved extra tablespoon oil only if the surface is dry. Do not chase browning.
  9. Build the aromatic base at 300°F. Leave only a thin film of oil. Add 6 rough-chopped garlic cloves, 1 Tbsp minced ginger, and the sliced Thai chile. Stir continuously for 15 to 20 seconds, just until fragrant.
  10. Finish in one uninterrupted toss. Return the blistered jalapeños and all pork. Toss 15 seconds. Stir Bowl P4 and pour it around the hot skillet. Toss constantly for 45 to 60 seconds. The liquid should boil immediately and turn into a glossy coating. Check the thickest representative pork slices; when they reach 145°F, remain flexible and tender, and no watery sauce pools underneath, turn the skillet off and remove the pork at once.
  11. Serve immediately. Spoon egg-scallion fried rice beside or under the pork. Give each serving plenty of blistered peppers and pork glaze without drowning the rice. Put extra chili crisp on the table for optional additional heat.

Final Taste Target

Fresh chile, garlic, pork, and savory heat should arrive first. The pork should be exceptionally soft and juicy with no tough edge. Green jalapeño gives grassy heat, red jalapeño gives ripe sweetness, Thai chile gives a sharp high note, and chili crisp plus chili garlic sauce add roasted depth. The glaze should be concentrated and glossy, never a heavy brown gravy. The fried rice should stay clean, eggy, scallion-forward, separated, and dry enough to catch the pork glaze.

Chef's Notes

The electric skillet holds heat after the dial changes. When the pork is done, move it out of the skillet rather than letting it sit on the hot surface.

If pork releases liquid during the precook, the skillet is crowded. Pull half out immediately and continue in smaller batches.

If the finishing sauce is still loose after the pork reaches 145°F, remove the pork and peppers first, reduce the sauce alone for 15 to 30 seconds, then toss everything through once.

For larger batches, scale pork ingredients proportionally by weight but keep each pork cooking load at about one pound or less so the velvet texture survives.
