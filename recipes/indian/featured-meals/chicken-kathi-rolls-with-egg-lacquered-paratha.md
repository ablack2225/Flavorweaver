# Chicken Kathi Rolls with Egg-Lacquered Paratha

> Scratch butter-layered paratha griddled with a thin egg lacquer, wrapped around deeply seasoned yogurt-spiced chicken thighs, crisp lettuce, tomato, purple onion, green onion, and a drizzleable cilantro-mint-jalapeño chutney dressing. This live-cooked version entered the Flavorweaver Hall of Fame after Don declared it better than falafel, his longtime favorite.

## Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

- **Chicken marinade** — Marinate at least **2 hours**, preferably overnight.
- **Paratha dough** — Mix earlier the same day. The dough can rest longer than the minimum; staggered breaks do not harm it as long as it stays covered.
- **Chutney dressing** — Make up to **1 day ahead** and refrigerate. Stir before serving and re-brighten with lime if needed.
- **House falafel sauces** — Optional but successful at the Hall of Fame service. Make ahead using [Don's Food-Truck Memory Falafel Naan Wraps](../../middle-eastern/featured-meals/dons-food-truck-memory-falafel-naan-wraps.md).
- **Fresh vegetables** — Cut close to service and keep undressed so they stay crisp.

## Service Countdown

- **Morning or night before** — Marinate chicken.
- **2 hours before serving** — Mix paratha dough and rest.
- **75 minutes before serving** — Make chutney dressing; prepare any optional house falafel sauces.
- **60 minutes before serving** — Laminate and coil parathas; cover and rest again.
- **35 minutes before serving** — Slice fresh vegetables and cook chicken; hold chicken warm loosely covered.
- **20 minutes before serving** — Roll and griddle parathas, egg-lacquer each one, fill, roll, and serve hot.

## Do Not Forget

- This is a **scratch paratha recipe**, not naan, tortilla, or a shortcut wrap.
- Rest the dough twice: after mixing and after coiling. Longer covered rests are acceptable and can make rolling easier.
- For visible layers: roll the first sheet thin, brush with a light even butter coat, accordion-pleat, gently stretch the strip, coil like a cinnamon roll, and tuck the tail underneath.
- Use gentle pressure during the final rolling so the laminated layers are not crushed.
- Amy's electric skillet runs hot. Start paratha and egg around **275°F** and chicken around **300°F**.
- Cook the first side of each paratha dry; add only a thin butter film after flipping so the milk solids do not scorch.
- Lift chicken from the marinade and leave excess marinade in the bowl. Do not pour leftover raw marinade into the skillet.
- The chicken is very thin and cooks quickly; protect it from overcooking.
- Keep the lettuce, tomato, and onions fresh and undressed. The chutney dressing and sauce bar supply the moisture and acidity.
- The egg layer should be thin and bonded to the paratha, not a scrambled egg filling.
- Serve the rolls hot and immediately after filling.

## Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

1. **Marinate the chicken.** In Bowl C2, whisk **1/3 cup yogurt**, **1 Tbsp ginger-garlic paste**, **1 Tbsp lime juice**, **1 Tbsp neutral oil**, **1 1/2 tsp garam masala**, **1 tsp cumin**, **1 tsp coriander**, **1 tsp paprika/Kashmiri chile**, **1/2 tsp turmeric**, **1/4–1/2 tsp cayenne**, **1 tsp kosher salt**, and **1/2 tsp black pepper**. Toss with **1 1/2 lb very thinly sliced chicken thighs**. Cover and marinate **2 hours to overnight**.

2. **Mix the butter paratha dough.** Whisk Bowl P1: **2 cups AP flour**, **3/4 tsp kosher salt**, and **1 tsp sugar**. Add **2 Tbsp warm melted butter** and most of the **3/4 cup warm water**. Mix until shaggy, then knead **6–8 minutes** until smooth, soft, and elastic. Add the held-back water gradually only if the dough feels dry. The dough should be slightly tacky, not stiff.

3. **Rest the dough.** Lightly butter or oil the surface, cover, and rest **45–60 minutes**. A longer covered rest is safe if physical breaks are needed.

4. **Make the chutney dressing.** Blend Bowl S1: **1 packed cup cilantro**, **1/4 packed cup mint**, **1 whole jalapeño with seeds and ribs**, **1 large garlic clove**, **3/4 tsp ginger**, **2 1/2 Tbsp lime juice**, **1 tsp honey/sugar**, **1/2 tsp kosher salt**, **1/2 tsp cumin**, optional **1/8–1/4 tsp cayenne**, and **1 Tbsp yogurt**. Add cold water **1 Tbsp at a time only as needed** until it becomes a smooth, drizzleable dressing. Taste for salt, heat, and lime.

5. **Prepare the fresh fill.** Shred **1 cup lettuce**. Remove the watery seed pulp from **1 large tomato** and dice the firm flesh. Thinly slice or finely dice **1/2 purple onion** and slice **2 green onions**. Keep everything undressed and chilled or cool until assembly.

6. **Divide and rest the dough balls.** Divide the rested dough into **4 equal balls**. Cover and rest **10–15 minutes** so they roll without springing back.

7. **Laminate and coil the parathas.** Roll one dough ball into a very thin **9–10 inch circle or oval**. Brush the full surface with about **1 1/2–2 tsp melted butter**; use enough to make it glossy without puddling. Starting at one edge, fold the dough back and forth in **3/4–1 inch accordion pleats**. Gently stretch the folded strip lengthwise, then wind it around itself like a cinnamon roll. Tuck the loose tail underneath. Repeat with all four, cover, and rest **20–30 minutes**; up to **60 minutes** is acceptable.

8. **Cook the chicken.** Heat Amy's electric skillet to about **300°F**. Add **1–2 tsp neutral oil** and spread into a thin film. Lift the chicken from the bowl, allowing excess marinade to remain behind, and cook in a single layer; use two batches if crowded. Leave the first side undisturbed for **60–90 seconds**, then toss and cook another **2–3 minutes**, just until no pink remains and scattered edges are browned. Finish with **1 Tbsp butter** and **1–2 tsp lime juice**. Hold warm loosely covered.

9. **Final-roll each paratha.** Place one rested coil spiral-side up, press gently with the palm, and roll from the center outward with light pressure, rotating frequently. Stop around **7–9 inches wide** and about **1/8 inch thick**. Do not force it paper-thin or crush the layers.

10. **Griddle each paratha.** Set the hot-running skillet around **275°F**. Lay one paratha onto the dry skillet. Cook **40–60 seconds** until bubbles appear and the underside develops light spots. Flip, brush with only a thin film of melted butter, and cook **30–45 seconds**. Flip once more and finish **30–60 seconds**, pressing pale areas gently, until lightly golden, layered, and flexible. Stack under a clean towel or inverted bowl.

11. **Egg-lacquer each paratha.** Beat **4 eggs** with a pinch of salt. For each paratha, smear the skillet with about **1/2 tsp melted butter**. Pour in approximately **1 beaten egg** and spread into a circle slightly smaller than the paratha. Immediately lay the cooked paratha over the wet egg and press gently. Cook **45–60 seconds**, until the egg bonds and sets, then flip for **10–20 seconds** to rewarm the plain side.

12. **Fill each roll.** Place the paratha egg-side up. Add a line of hot chicken first, drizzle with cilantro-mint chutney dressing, then add fresh lettuce, tomato, purple onion, and green onion. Add any optional falafel sauces at the table rather than overloading the wrap. Finish with lime.

13. **Roll and serve.** Fold the bottom slightly over the filling, tuck the sides if desired, and roll firmly. Set seam-side down. Serve immediately with extra chutney dressing, lime wedges, and the optional house sauce bar.

## Electric Skillet Notes

- **Chicken:** Start around **300°F** with a thin film of neutral oil. The very thin strips should cook in approximately **3–4 minutes total** after hitting the skillet.
- **Paratha:** Start around **275°F** because butter milk solids can scorch. Cook the first side dry and butter only after flipping.
- **Egg lacquer:** Use **250–275°F** if the egg browns before it bonds.
- **Between batches:** Wipe away dark butter solids so later parathas do not taste burnt.

## Final Taste Target

The finished roll should be hot, buttery, layered, juicy, deeply spiced, herb-bright, and fresh: flexible flaky paratha, a thin savory egg layer, intensely seasoned chicken, crisp undressed vegetables, vivid jalapeño-cilantro-mint dressing, and optional sauce-bar contrast. It must taste like a complete Hall of Fame kathi roll, never a generic chicken wrap.

## Chef's Notes

- The chicken flavor is canon-locked. Don singled it out as the star and said the complete roll surpassed falafel, his longtime favorite.
- The tested fresh vegetables were better for this build than the original lime-dressed onion crunch because the loose chutney dressing and sauce bar already supplied acid and moisture.
- Melted butter successfully replaced ghee in the dough, lamination, griddle, and egg steps. Protect the lower skillet temperature and thin application.
- The paratha is physically demanding but tolerant of breaks. Keep dough and coils covered; longer rests can improve relaxation and do not reduce quality.
- The sauce bar is a successful service option, not a mandatory part of the core recipe.

**What worked:**

- Don said the rolls were better than falafel, his longtime favorite.

- Scratch butter-laminated paratha with the accordion-folded coil method.
