# Korean Smoky-Plum BBQ Pork Hoagies

> Two soft homemade hoagie rolls filled with overnight-marinated pork butt that is hard-seared, gently braised until pull-apart tender, rested in its juices, coarse-pulled, and lacquered with a smoky fresh-plum gochujang barbecue glaze. Spicy mayo provides creamy heat while cold kosher dill pickles and paper-thin purple onion provide clean acidity and bite without turning the sandwich into a dressed slaw.

## Advance Prep

- **Already complete:** marinate the 2 lb pork butt for 8-24 hours.
- **Frozen rolls:** thaw and proof only 2 shaped hoagie rolls for tonight's service.
- **Plum glaze:** may be made during the braise or up to 2 days ahead.
- **Spicy mayo:** may be made up to 2 days ahead and kept refrigerated.
- **Do not slice the purple onion too early:** slice close to service so it stays crisp and fresh.
- **Do not leave pickles wet:** slice and pat them dry before building.

## Non-Negotiable Hoagie Texture Standard

This is not a recipe for intact glazed pork chunks. A hoagie needs pork that yields cleanly with every bite.

- Braise until a fork enters with almost no resistance and twisting begins to separate the meat.
- Rest the pork covered in its braising liquid for 20-30 minutes.
- Pull into substantial irregular pieces, not dry hairlike shreds.
- Restore reduced pork liquid before adding plum glaze.
- Reserve the extra pork before the final broiler lacquer.
- Broil only the two-hoagie portion long enough to create lacquered edges.
- Do not simmer pulled pork in thick glaze; sugar-rich sauce will tighten and scorch.

## Measured Ingredients by Component

### A. Pork and Overnight Marinade

- 2 lb pork butt, cut into 1 1/2- to 2-inch chunks
- 2 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce
- 2 Tbsp gochujang
- 1 Tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar
- 1 Tbsp honey
- 1/3 cup finely grated sweet onion with its juices
- 6 cloves garlic, grated or finely minced
- 1 Tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 2 green onions, whites and light greens thinly sliced; reserve dark tops
- 1 tsp gochugaru, or 1/2 tsp crushed red pepper
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1/2 tsp MSG
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil

### B. Savory Braise

- 1 Tbsp neutral oil or olive oil
- 1/2 large sweet onion, about 1 cup sliced 1/4 inch thick
- 3/4 cup lower-sodium chicken broth
- 1/2 cup water, plus a little hot water only if needed
- 1 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce
- 1 Tbsp gochujang
- 1 Tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar

### C. Charred Fresh-Plum BBQ Glaze

- 1 1/2 lb fresh plums, skins on, halved and pitted
- 2 Tbsp gochujang
- 3 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce
- 1/4 cup unseasoned rice vinegar, plus more for final balancing
- 3 Tbsp honey
- Up to 1 Tbsp light brown sugar; omit or reduce if plums are sweet
- 1 Tbsp tomato paste
- 1 Tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 4 cloves garlic, grated or minced
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp gochugaru, or 1 finely minced hot red chile
- 1/2 cup water
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil, added off heat

### D. Spicy Gochujang Mayo — Two-Hoagie Batch

- 1/4 cup mayonnaise
- 2 tsp gochujang
- 1 tsp fresh lime juice
- 1/2 tsp unseasoned rice vinegar
- 1/2 tsp honey
- Small pinch garlic powder
- 1/8 tsp toasted sesame oil
- Pinch gochugaru, optional
- Pinch fine salt only if needed

### E. Kosher Dill and Purple Onion Finish

- 6-10 thin kosher dill pickle slices, or 2 pickle spears cut into thin diagonal or lengthwise slices
- 1/4 small purple onion, sliced into nearly transparent half-moons or wispy layers
- 1 green onion, thinly sliced, optional
- 1/2 tsp toasted sesame seeds, optional

**Preparation standard:** Pat the pickles very dry. Keep the purple onion raw and paper-thin. If the onion is unusually harsh, rinse or soak it in ice water for only 5 minutes, then drain and dry thoroughly. Do not pickle it and do not dress the topping.

### F. Hoagie Rolls and Tonight's Build

- 2 frozen shaped, unproofed soft buttery hoagie rolls, about 135-150 g each
- 1 Tbsp melted butter, divided between warm tops and cut-side toasting
- 10-12 oz finished pork total, about 5-6 oz per sandwich
- Spicy gochujang mayo
- Kosher dill pickle slices
- Paper-thin purple onion
- Clean smoky-plum sauce, used sparingly only if the pork needs more shine
- Reserved green onion tops and toasted sesame seeds, optional
- Lime wedges, optional

### G. Reserved Pork Batch

- Remaining coarse-pulled pork, approximately 2 additional sandwich portions
- 1-2 Tbsp reduced braising liquid per reserved portion
- Extra clean smoky-plum sauce packed separately

- **Tray 1 — Broiled Plums:** half the pitted plums, cut side up.
- **Pot 2 — Plum Base:** remaining plums and all measured glaze ingredients except sesame oil.
- **Bowl 3 — Spicy Mayo:** two-hoagie batch whisked smooth.
- **Plate 4 — Cold Finish:** dry kosher dill slices and paper-thin purple onion kept separate until assembly.

### Service and Storage

- **Tray 1 — Two Proofed Rolls:** covered until baking.
- **Bowl 2 — Clean Plum Glaze:** sealed before braising liquid is handled.

- **Container 4 — Reserved Pork:** extra pork moistened with reduced braising liquid, cooled promptly.
- **Container 5 — Extra Clean Sauce:** packed separately from the pork.

## Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

1. **Begin thawing and proofing 2 rolls.** Place the two frozen shaped hoagie rolls on a parchment-lined tray with space between them. Protect them with lightly oiled plastic wrap or place the tray inside a food-safe bag. Let thaw and proof at comfortable room temperature until fully thawed, visibly expanded and puffy, and a lightly floured fingertip dent returns slowly. This may take 3-5 hours. If ready early, refrigerate covered for up to about 1 hour.

2. **Temper and dry the pork.** Remove the marinated pork from the refrigerator 30-40 minutes before searing. Lift the chunks from the marinade, let excess marinade drip away, and lightly pat the surfaces dry. Discard all raw marinade. Heat the oven to 315°F.

3. **Sear in batches.** Heat a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high to high heat. Add enough oil to film the bottom. Sear the pork in 2-3 uncrowded batches. Leave each side untouched long enough to form a deep brown crust before turning. Transfer browned pork to a tray; do not cook the centers through.

4. **Build the braise.** Lower heat to medium. Add the sliced onion and cook in the rendered pork fat for about 5 minutes, until softened and browned at the edges. Pour in the whisked broth, water, soy, gochujang, and vinegar. Scrape up every browned bit.

5. **Cover and braise for pull-apart tenderness.** Return pork and accumulated juices to the pot. Liquid should reach one-third to halfway up the chunks. Add a small amount of hot water only if needed. Cover tightly and braise at 315°F. Begin checking around 2 hours 15 minutes, then every 20 minutes. Expect roughly 2 1/2-3 1/4 hours. The pork is ready when a fork enters with almost no resistance and twisting begins to separate the meat. Internal temperature will commonly be around 198-205°F.

6. **Char half the plums during the braise.** Arrange half the pitted plums cut side up on a foil-lined rimmed pan. Broil 6-10 minutes until the skins blister and the cut surfaces show dark caramelized spots.

7. **Build the plum glaze.** Combine the broiled plums and their juices with the uncharred plums, gochujang, soy, rice vinegar, honey, optional brown sugar, tomato paste, ginger, garlic, smoked paprika, gochugaru, and water. Simmer uncovered over medium-low heat 20-30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the fruit collapses.

8. **Blend, strain, and reduce.** Blend until completely smooth. Press through a fine-mesh strainer into a clean saucepan. Reduce gently until the sauce coats the back of a spoon and yields about 1 3/4-2 cups. Remove from heat and stir in sesame oil. It should taste savory and fermented first, then plum-bright, smoky, sweet-hot, and acidic—not like jam. Add vinegar 1 tsp at a time if needed.

9. **Make the two-hoagie spicy mayo.** Whisk all mayo ingredients until smooth. Chill at least 30 minutes. Taste it on bread rather than from the spoon; it should provide creamy heat without overpowering the plum pork.

10. **Rest the pork in its liquid.** When fully tender, remove the Dutch oven from the oven but keep it tightly covered. Let the pork rest in the braising liquid 20-30 minutes. Raise the oven to 350°F for the rolls.

11. **Bake the 2 proofed rolls.** Remove the protective plastic. Bake at 350°F for 18-22 minutes, until evenly golden and 190-195°F internally. Brush the tops lightly with melted butter while hot. Cool at least 20 minutes before slicing.

12. **Coarse-pull the pork.** Lift the rested pork into a wide bowl or tray. Break it into short, substantial shreds and small tender chunks. Do not finely shred it. Cover loosely while reducing the liquid.

13. **Reduce the braising liquid.** Strain the liquid into a saucepan, pressing lightly on the onion. Skim excess fat if needed and boil until concentrated to about 1/3 cup. Taste before using because soy and gochujang concentrate with reduction.

14. **Restore moisture to the full batch.** Fold about 1/4 cup reduced braising liquid into all the coarse-pulled pork. The meat should look juicy before any plum glaze is added.

15. **Divide before lacquering.** Measure 10-12 oz moistened pork for tonight's two hoagies. Place the remaining pork into storage portions with 1-2 Tbsp reduced braising liquid per portion. Cool promptly. Refrigerate up to 3 days or freeze. Keep extra clean plum sauce separate.

16. **Build tonight's lacquer.** Fold about 1/3 cup clean plum glaze into the 10-12 oz service pork. For the broiler finish, whisk another 2 Tbsp clean glaze with 1 Tbsp reduced pork liquid.

17. **Lacquer and char the service portion.** Spread the pork on a foil-lined broiler-safe pan in a loose layer. Apply half the lacquer and broil 2-4 minutes until upper edges darken. Turn sections gently, add the remaining lacquer in a thin coat, and broil another 1-3 minutes. Stop when sticky charred edges appear while most of the meat remains soft and juicy.

18. **Prepare the cold finish.** Cut kosher dill pickles into thin slices and pat them very dry. Slice 1/4 small purple onion into nearly transparent half-moons or wispy layers. Keep them raw and undressed. Use the optional 5-minute ice-water rinse only if the onion is unusually harsh.

19. **Butter-toast the rolls.** Split each cooled roll lengthwise without cutting entirely through the hinge. Brush the cut faces lightly with melted butter. Toast cut-side down in a hot skillet until golden and crisp while leaving the exterior soft.

20. **Build each hoagie.** Spread spicy mayo over both toasted cut faces. Add 5-6 oz hot lacquered pork. Add no more than 1-2 tsp clean plum glaze only if the pork needs more shine. Layer on 3-5 dry kosher dill slices and a modest nest of paper-thin purple onion. Finish with optional scallion tops and sesame seeds. Serve immediately with the separately developed sides.

## Troubleshooting and Rescue Notes

| Problem | Correction |
|---|---|
| Pork is still tough | Re-cover and continue braising; check every 20 minutes. |
| Pork is tender but dry | Fold in warm reduced braising liquid 1 Tbsp at a time before adding glaze. |
| Plum glaze tastes jammy | Add rice vinegar 1 tsp at a time, then a small splash of water or soy if needed. |
| Plum glaze burns under broiler | Use thinner coats, shorten broiler passes, and move the tray farther from the element. |
| Mayo tastes too hot | Add mayonnaise 1 Tbsp at a time and a few drops of lime. |
| Pickles wet the bread | Pat every slice dry before assembly and place them above the pork rather than against the bottom crumb. |
| Purple onion overwhelms the sandwich | Use fewer paper-thin layers or give them a 5-minute ice-water rinse, then dry thoroughly. |
| Sandwich tastes too sweet | Add another dry dill slice or a few wisps of onion; do not add more plum glaze. |
| Sandwich tastes too busy | Remove optional garnish before changing the pork, mayo, pickles, or onion. |
| Rolls are proofed before pork is ready | Refrigerate covered up to about 1 hour, then let stand while pork finishes. |

## Chef's Notes

The sandwich no longer uses a dressed vegetable crunch. That layer duplicated the spicy-sweet complexity already present in the plum barbecue sauce and gochujang mayo. The cleaner finish is stronger: cold kosher dill supplies salt, garlic-dill acidity, and snap; raw paper-thin purple onion supplies fresh bite.

The defining contrast is now **hot juicy-charred pork + cool sharp pickles + crisp raw onion + creamy spicy mayo + warm butter-toasted bread**. Every layer has a separate job.

The full pork batch remains intentional because the meat was marinated before the two-hoagie service decision. Reserve the extra pork after restoring it with braising liquid but before adding the sugar-rich plum lacquer. This preserves moisture and makes the leftovers more versatile.

The sauce still uses the house two-plum treatment: half broiled for roast and smoke, half uncharred for brighter fruit character. Straining creates a polished lacquer rather than a fibrous fruit sauce.
