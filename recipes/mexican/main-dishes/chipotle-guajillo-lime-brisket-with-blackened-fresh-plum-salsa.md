# Chipotle-Guajillo Lime Brisket with Blackened Fresh-Plum Salsa

> Thin brisket is coated in a cooked, strained guajillo-chipotle marinade, seared in small batches until deeply browned, and finished in a clean smoky chile-lime pan glaze. A required large-batch salsa combines skillet-blackened plums, tomatoes, onion, garlic, and fresh chiles with uncooked plums, cilantro, lime, and a guajillo-honey vinegar reduction for juicy, charred, sweet-hot contrast.

## Prep Notes

- **Brisket cut:** Thin slicing across the grain is essential. Long grain-aligned strips will remain chewy regardless of marinade.
- **Marination:** The cooked chile sauce carries flavor without exposing the beef to prolonged raw acid. Once lime juice is added, limit marination to 30-90 minutes.
- **Clean sauce rule:** Reserve 1/2 cup master sauce before it touches raw brisket. Discard the raw-meat marinade.
- **Salsa texture:** Use the food processor in short pulses. The finished salsa should be textured and spoonable, never a plum smoothie.
- **Tomatoes:** Keep the seeds and tomato gel. They contain flavor; excess liquid can be drained from the finished portion used on sandwiches.
- **Blackening method:** Use a very hot dry skillet and work in batches. Remove each ingredient when deeply blistered; garlic finishes earlier than plums, tomatoes, and onion.
- **Equipment:** Heavy skillet or electric skillet, saucepan, blender, fine-mesh strainer, food processor, and sheet pan or large tray for staged ingredients.

## Stage-Based Prep Bowls

### Stage 1 — Master Chile Sauce

- **Tray 1 — Dry Chiles:** 3 stemmed and seeded guajillos.
- **Skillet Bowl — Char Aromatics:** 1 cup onion wedges and 5 skin-on garlic cloves.
- **Blender Bowl:** Rehydrated guajillos, peeled charred garlic, charred onion, 1 chipotle plus 1 Tbsp adobo, 1/3 cup orange juice, 1/4 cup soaking liquid or broth, lime zest, 2 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 Tbsp honey, cumin, coriander, oregano, smoked paprika, black pepper, MSG, and 1 Tbsp olive oil.

### Stage 2 — Brisket

- **Bag or Bowl 1 — Marinade:** 3/4 cup cooled master sauce, 1 Tbsp lime juice, and 1/2 tsp fine salt.
- **Tray 2 — Brisket:** 1 1/2 lb thin-sliced brisket, drained and lightly patted before searing.
- **Bowl 3 — Clean Finish:** 1/2 cup reserved master sauce, 2-4 Tbsp water or broth, and 1-2 Tbsp lime juice held separately.

### Stage 3 — Salsa Reduction and Blackened Base

- **Small Pan — Reduction:** 1 toasted guajillo, 3 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp white vinegar, and 1 Tbsp plus 1 tsp honey.
- **Skillet Tray — Blacken:** Half the plums, 2 Roma tomatoes, 1/2 onion, 1 jalapeño, 1/2-1 fresh red chile, and 3 skin-on garlic cloves.
- **Fresh Bowl:** Remaining fresh plums, 1/2 raw jalapeño, 2 green onions, 1/2 cup cilantro, 2 Tbsp lime juice, 2 tsp olive oil, 3/4 tsp salt, and 1/4 tsp MSG.

### Stage 4 — Service

- **Bowl 1 — Salsa:** Finished blackened fresh-plum salsa; lightly drain only the portion used in bread.
- **Bowl 2 — Optional Vegetables:** Charred onion and bell pepper.
- **Bowl 3 — Final Finish:** Remaining lime wedges, cilantro or green onion, and extra clean chile sauce if desired.

## Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

1. **Make the guajillo-honey reduction for the salsa.** Toast **1 stemmed and seeded guajillo** in a dry skillet for about **10-15 seconds per side**, only until fragrant. Tear it into a small saucepan with **3 Tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp white vinegar, and 1 Tbsp plus 1 tsp honey**. Simmer gently for **4-6 minutes**, until reduced by roughly half. Remove from heat, steep **5 minutes**, strain, and cool.

2. **Blacken the salsa produce in batches.** Heat a heavy dry skillet over high heat. Blacken half of the **1 1/2 lb plums**, cut side down first; both halved Roma tomatoes; **1/2 onion** in thick wedges; **1 whole jalapeño**; **1/2 fresh hot red chile** to begin; and **3 skin-on garlic cloves**. Turn only as needed so the produce develops genuine dark blistering and blackened patches. Remove the garlic when softened and browned; continue cooking the larger produce until charred but not collapsed. Cool completely and peel the garlic.

3. **Process the blackened salsa foundation.** Add the blackened tomatoes, onion, peeled garlic, charred jalapeño, charred red chile, and charred plums to a food processor with about **two-thirds of the cooled guajillo-honey reduction, 3/4 tsp fine salt, and 1/4 tsp MSG**. Pulse until finely chopped and cohesive, scraping the bowl as needed.

4. **Add the salsa's fresh layer.** Add the remaining fresh plums, up to **1/2 raw jalapeño**, **2 sliced green onions, 1/2 cup cilantro, 2 Tbsp fresh lime juice, and 2 tsp olive oil**. Pulse in short bursts only until textured and spoonable. Rest **15-20 minutes**, then taste in this order: salt, lime, chile heat, and sweet-acid balance. Add the remaining reduction, another tablespoon lime juice, more red chile, or the remaining 1/4 tsp salt only as needed. Refrigerate while the brisket is prepared.

5. **Toast and rehydrate the brisket guajillos.** Wipe the skillet clean. Toast **3 guajillos** for **10-15 seconds per side** until fragrant and flexible; do not let them blacken. Cover with very hot water and soak **15 minutes**. Reserve at least **1/4 cup strained soaking liquid**.

6. **Char the sauce aromatics.** In the dry hot skillet, blacken **1 cup sweet-onion wedges** and **5 skin-on garlic cloves** until the onion has dark edges and the garlic is softened. Peel the garlic.

7. **Blend and cook the master chile sauce.** Blend the rehydrated guajillos, charred onion, peeled garlic, **1 chipotle plus 1 Tbsp adobo sauce, 1/3 cup orange juice, 1/4 cup soaking liquid or broth, zest of 1 lime, 2 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce, 1 Tbsp honey, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander, 1 tsp Mexican oregano, 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/2 tsp black pepper, 1/2 tsp MSG, and 1 Tbsp olive oil** until completely smooth. Strain into a saucepan and simmer **6-8 minutes**, stirring frequently, until the raw chile aroma disappears and the sauce lightly coats a spoon. Cool to room temperature.

8. **Divide the clean sauce and marinate.** Reserve **1/2 cup master sauce** in a clean container. Combine **3/4 cup sauce, 1 Tbsp lime juice, and 1/2 tsp fine salt** with **1 1/2 lb thin-sliced brisket**. Refrigerate **30-90 minutes**.

9. **Drain and stage the brisket.** Remove the meat from the marinade and let the excess drip away. Pat lightly so the surfaces are tacky rather than wet. Discard the raw marinade. Separate tangled slices before they reach the hot skillet.

10. **Char the optional vegetables.** Heat the skillet or electric skillet until very hot, approximately **375-400°F** if temperature-controlled. Add **1 tsp tallow or oil**, then char the onion wedges and bell-pepper strips in an uncrowded layer. Season with a pinch of salt and move to a warm tray.

11. **Sear the brisket in small batches.** Add enough of the remaining tallow or oil to keep a thin film on the skillet. Sear the brisket in **3-4 batches**, placing each slice or small cluster in direct contact with the surface. Let the first side brown before tossing or turning. Cook only until dark edges form and the meat is just cooked through, generally **1-3 minutes per batch** depending on thickness. Transfer each batch to the warm tray.

12. **Build the clean pan glaze.** Lower heat to medium. Add **1/2 cup reserved clean master sauce** and **2 Tbsp water or low-sodium broth**, scraping up the browned fond. Simmer until glossy and slightly thickened, adding the remaining 1-2 Tbsp liquid only if needed. Turn off the heat and add **1 Tbsp fresh lime juice**.

13. **Glaze without overcooking.** Return the brisket and any accumulated juices to the pan. Toss for only **30-60 seconds**, until every piece is glossy. Fold in the charred onion and pepper if using. Taste with a spoonful of salsa; add more lime or a small pinch of salt only after judging the full bite.

14. **Rest and serve.** Rest the glazed brisket **5-10 minutes**. Serve with the blackened fresh-plum salsa in soft sub rolls, flatbread wraps, rice bowls, tacos, or lettuce cups. Lightly drain salsa used inside bread, but keep the released juices for bowls or table service.

## Troubleshooting / Rescue Notes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Brisket will not brown | Dry it more thoroughly, raise heat, and sear fewer slices at a time |
| Brisket is chewy | Confirm it was cut across grain; chop it into shorter pieces and warm briefly in the glaze rather than continuing to fry it dry |
| Chile sauce tastes bitter | A guajillo was scorched; soften with orange juice, honey, and broth, but remake if the bitterness is dominant |
| Pan glaze is too salty | Loosen with unsalted broth or water and fresh lime; do not correct salt with sugar alone |
| Glaze is too thin | Remove the meat and reduce the clean sauce separately before returning it |
| Salsa became puree | Fold in additional hand-chopped fresh plum, onion, cilantro, and lime |
| Salsa is watery | Let it rest, then drain only the serving portion needed for sandwiches; retain juices for bowls |
| Salsa tastes flat | Add salt first, then lime; fruit salsas often seem bland when simply under-salted |
| Salsa is too sweet | Add lime or rice vinegar in small increments; do not add more smoked spices |
| Salsa is too hot | Add more fresh plum or tomato and a small pinch of salt |

## Chef's Notes

The recipe deliberately keeps most lime juice out of the marinade. Lime zest carries citrus perfume without tightening thin brisket, while fresh juice added to the clean glaze remains vivid after cooking.

The salsa does not need cumin, smoked paprika, or chili-powder blends. Those flavors already live in the brisket. Its job is to provide blackened produce depth, fresh fruit, raw herbaceousness, acid, and clean chile heat.

The tomato seeds and gel remain because they carry flavor. For sandwiches, control moisture after processing rather than discarding flavorful tomato liquid before cooking.

<summary>Flavorweaver Direction</summary>

This build separates the meat's dark chile-smoke architecture from the salsa's fresh and blackened fruit architecture. Guajillo appears in both components as a quiet bridge, but cumin and smoked paprika remain out of the salsa so the two components do not collapse into one flavor.

The key restaurant-level controls are a cooked and strained master chile sauce, clean-sauce reservation, short acid exposure for thin brisket, uncrowded high-heat searing, a separate pan reduction, two-state fruit, ingredient-by-ingredient blackening, and pulse-controlled food-processor texture.
