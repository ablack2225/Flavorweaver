# Red-Chile Three-Cheese Enchiladas

> Homemade corn tortillas rolled around sharp cheddar, pepper jack, mozzarella, and finely diced onion, then baked beneath a silky guajillo-enriched red-chile gravy. The finished enchiladas should taste distinctly of chile and cheese—not canned sauce, queso dip, or casserole.

## Prep Notes

- **Ingredient temperatures:** Thaw tortillas completely. Cold or partially frozen tortillas will crack during rolling.
- **Cheese:** Shred from blocks for the smoothest melt. Pre-shredded cheese can remain dry and separate because of anti-caking starch.
- **Onion cut:** Dice the filling onion very finely so it softens during the short bake without releasing large amounts of water.
- **Equipment:** Small saucepan, blender, skillet, tongs, and an approximately 8-by-8-inch baking dish.
- **Sauce consistency:** The gravy should pour easily and lightly coat a spoon. It must not be as thick as queso dip.
- **Holding limit:** Best served within 10-15 minutes of leaving the oven.

## Instructions

1. **Heat the oven and stage the dish.** Heat the oven to **375°F**. Set an approximately **8-by-8-inch baking dish** beside the stove. Shred **1 cup sharp cheddar, 3/4 cup pepper jack, and 1/2 cup low-moisture mozzarella**. Mix the cheeses, reserve **3/4 cup** for topping, and leave **1 1/2 cups** for filling. Very finely dice **1/3 cup onion**.

2. **Toast and soak the guajillo.** Heat a dry skillet over medium. Press **1 stemmed and seeded guajillo chile** against the pan for about **10-15 seconds per side**, only until fragrant and flexible. Do not blacken it. Cover with very hot water and soak for **10 minutes**, then drain.

3. **Blend the red-chile base.** Blend the softened guajillo with **10 oz red enchilada sauce, 1/2 cup low-sodium broth, and 1 peeled garlic clove** until completely smooth, about **45-60 seconds**. For the silkiest gravy, strain the mixture through a medium-mesh sieve.

4. **Cook the light roux and bloom the spices.** Heat **1 Tbsp neutral oil or unsalted butter** in a small saucepan over medium-low. Whisk in **1 Tbsp all-purpose flour** and cook for **60-90 seconds** without browning. Add **1/2 tsp cumin, 1/2 tsp crushed Mexican oregano, and 1/4 tsp black pepper**; stir for **15 seconds**.

5. **Finish the elevated red-chile gravy.** Slowly whisk the blended chile mixture into the roux. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook **8-10 minutes**, stirring frequently, until the raw canned-sauce edge is gone and the gravy lightly coats a spoon. If it becomes too thick, add broth **1 Tbsp at a time**. Taste before salting. Add up to **1/4 tsp sugar** only if the sauce tastes harsh—not automatically.

6. **Prepare the baking dish.** Spread **1/2 cup finished red-chile gravy** across the bottom of the baking dish. Keep the remaining sauce warm over the lowest heat.

7. **Condition the tortillas.** Heat a skillet over medium and add only enough of the **1-2 Tbsp neutral oil** to leave a thin sheen. Working one at a time, warm each thawed tortilla for about **5-8 seconds per side**. The tortilla should become hot, supple, and slightly protected by oil without frying crisp. Stack the warmed tortillas under a clean towel.

8. **Fill and roll all six enchiladas.** Place **1/4 cup filling cheese** down the center of each warm tortilla and scatter about **2 1/2 tsp finely diced onion** over the cheese. Roll gently but snugly. Arrange seam-side down in the prepared dish. Work while the tortillas remain warm; rewarm briefly if any begin to stiffen.

9. **Sauce and top.** Spoon the remaining red-chile gravy evenly over the six enchiladas, concentrating it over the seams and allowing the outermost edges to remain partly visible. Do not flood the dish. Scatter the reserved **3/4 cup topping cheese** evenly over the center line.

10. **Bake.** Bake uncovered at **375°F for 15-18 minutes**, until the sauce bubbles around the edges and the cheese is completely melted. For browned spots, broil for only **30-60 seconds**, watching continuously.

11. **Rest and finish.** Rest the enchiladas for **5 minutes** so the molten cheese settles and the sauce lightly tightens. Finish with diced onion or green onion, cilantro, jalapeño, or sour cream as desired. Serve lime wedges alongside rather than squeezing heavily over the entire dish.

## Do Not Forget

- The guajillo is toasted only until fragrant. Burnt guajillo makes the full sauce bitter.
- Reserve **3/4 cup cheese before filling** so the topping amount is protected.
- Warm tortillas in a thin oil film only until pliable; crisp tortillas will crack when rolled.
- Use enough sauce to coat and protect the tortillas, but do not drown them.
- Stop baking as soon as the filling is melted and the sauce bubbles; extended baking can make cheddar and pepper jack release oil.

## Final Taste Target

The first bite should lead with toasted red chile and sharp cheddar, followed by creamy pepper jack, gentle mozzarella stretch, and a clean onion bite. The sauce should taste deeper and silkier than it did from the can but remain clearly red-chile gravy—not tomato sauce, queso, or mole. The tortillas should be tender and sauced while retaining enough structure to roll and serve cleanly.

## Troubleshooting / Rescue Notes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sauce tastes canned or flat | Simmer 3-5 minutes longer; then adjust salt before considering a tiny pinch of sugar |
| Sauce is too thick | Whisk in warm broth 1 Tbsp at a time |
| Sauce is too thin | Simmer uncovered, stirring frequently, until it lightly coats a spoon |
| Tortillas crack | Rewarm briefly under a damp towel or in the lightly oiled skillet |
| Cheese filling looks oily | The enchiladas baked too long or the heat was too aggressive; pull as soon as melted and bubbling |
| Finished dish is too heavy | Add fresh onion, cilantro, jalapeño, or a restrained spoon of sour cream; serve lime alongside |

## Chef's Notes

- This recipe intentionally leaves the Velveeta Queso Blanco and Hot Rotel out. They are useful pantry ingredients, but combining queso, Rotel, red enchilada sauce, and three cheeses would blur the red-chile architecture.
- The cheese blend is calibrated by role: sharp cheddar for identity, pepper jack for heat and melt, and mozzarella for cohesion and stretch.
- The canned enchilada sauce is treated as a foundation. Guajillo, garlic, broth, a light roux, and a proper simmer remove the one-note canned quality without turning the recipe into an all-day chile sauce.
