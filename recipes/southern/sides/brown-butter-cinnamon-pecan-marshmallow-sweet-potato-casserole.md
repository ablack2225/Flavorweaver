# Brown-Butter Cinnamon-Pecan Marshmallow Sweet Potato Casserole

> A fully loaded Southern holiday sweet potato casserole built on deeply roasted sweet potatoes, nutty brown butter, dark brown sugar, maple, warm spice, and an intentionally noticeable black-pepper backbone, then crowned with cinnamon-pecan streusel and deeply toasted marshmallows.

## Advance Prep

- Roast the sweet potatoes several hours ahead if desired. Cool just enough to handle, scoop the flesh, and refrigerate covered once no longer steaming.
- The filling may be mixed and spread into the buttered baking dish up to 24 hours ahead.
- Keep the streusel in a separate covered container until baking so it stays crumbly.
- Keep marshmallows and the final reserved cinnamon pecans separate until the last few minutes.
- If the assembled filling is refrigerator-cold, add about **10 to 15 minutes** to the covered warming stage before the streusel bake.

## Do Not Forget

- Roast the sweet potatoes **whole** so the flesh stays concentrated rather than waterlogged.
- In the non-convection oven, use a **wire rack over a foil-lined sheet pan** for more even airflow and easy cleanup.
- Roast until the potatoes are **fully collapsing-soft**, not merely fork-tender.
- Taste the seasoned sweet-potato filling **before adding the eggs**. The black pepper should be clearly present and the salt should sharpen the potato.
- Do not overmix once the eggs are in; the target is plush and silky, not gluey.
- Keep the cinnamon candied pecans out of the broiler. Their sugar coating can scorch quickly.
- Broil the marshmallows under constant watch. Deep bronze with a few dark edges is the target.
- The final casserole should be sweet and indulgent, but the salt and pepper must keep it from reading like dessert.

## Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

1. **Roast the sweet potatoes whole.** Heat the oven to **400°F** with a rack in the center. Set a wire rack over a foil-lined rimmed sheet pan. Scrub and dry **3 lb sweet potatoes**, pierce each several times with a fork, and place them on the rack. Roast until completely soft, visibly slumped, and beginning to release sticky syrup, about **55 to 75 minutes** depending on size. A knife should slide through with almost no resistance.

2. **Rest and scoop.** Let the potatoes cool **10 to 15 minutes**, just until safe to handle. Split them and scoop the flesh into a large mixing bowl. Discard the skins.

3. **Brown the butter.** While the potatoes rest, melt **8 Tbsp unsalted butter** over medium heat. Continue cooking, swirling often, until the milk solids turn amber and the butter smells deeply nutty. Immediately remove from heat so the solids do not burn.

4. **Build the seasoned mash.** Mash the warm sweet-potato flesh until mostly smooth. Add the browned butter, **1/3 cup dark brown sugar**, **2 Tbsp maple syrup**, **1/3 cup heavy cream**, **2 tsp vanilla**, **1 tsp cinnamon**, **1/4 tsp nutmeg**, **1 tsp kosher salt**, and **1 tsp freshly ground black pepper**. Mix until silky.

5. **Taste before the eggs.** Taste the filling now. It should be richly sweet-potato-forward, buttery and warm-spiced, with an unmistakable peppery finish and enough salt to make the sweetness pop. Adjust salt or pepper now if needed.

6. **Add the eggs.** Let the mash stand about **5 minutes** if it is still piping hot. Whisk in **2 lightly beaten eggs** until fully incorporated but do not beat aggressively.

7. **Fill the casserole dish.** Reduce the oven to **350°F**. Butter a **9x13-inch baking dish** and spread the sweet-potato filling evenly into it.

8. **Make the cinnamon-pecan streusel.** Finely chop **1 cup cinnamon candied pecans** and reserve **1/4 cup** for the finish. Mix the remaining **3/4 cup pecans** with **1/3 cup dark brown sugar**, **1/3 cup all-purpose flour**, and **1/4 tsp kosher salt**. Stir in **4 Tbsp melted butter** until damp, irregular crumbs form.

9. **Bake the casserole.** Scatter the streusel evenly over the filling. Bake at **350°F for 25 to 30 minutes**, until the center is hot, the edges are lightly puffed, and the streusel smells toasted. If the filling started refrigerator-cold, cover loosely with foil for the first 20 minutes and expect roughly **35 to 45 minutes total** before the marshmallow stage.

10. **Add the marshmallow crown.** Remove the casserole and switch the oven to broil. Scatter **3 cups mini marshmallows** generously across the surface, leaving a little streusel visible between clusters.

11. **Broil hard and watch constantly.** Return the casserole to the oven and broil just until the marshmallows are swollen and deeply golden with a few dark toasted edges, usually **1 to 3 minutes**. Do not walk away.

12. **Finish with cinnamon pecans.** Immediately scatter the reserved **1/4 cup cinnamon candied pecans** over the hot marshmallow crown. They should stay fragrant and crisp rather than going under the broiler.

13. **Rest and serve.** Let the casserole stand **10 minutes** before serving so the filling settles and the streusel firms slightly. Serve warm.

## Final Taste Target

The first bite should read as unmistakable Thanksgiving sweet potato casserole: silky, buttery, sweet, cinnamon-warm, pecan-rich, and marshmallow-toasted. Then the Flavorweaver difference should arrive: brown-butter depth, concentrated roasted sweet potato, a real black-pepper presence, and enough salt to sharpen the sweetness instead of letting it become cloying. The topping should move from crisp cinnamon-pecan streusel to soft, bronzed marshmallow with fresh crunchy pecans on top.

## Troubleshooting / Rescue Notes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Filling tastes flat before eggs | Add another small pinch kosher salt and a few grinds black pepper; the pepper should be noticeable |
| Filling is too sweet | Add 1 to 2 Tbsp heavy cream plus a pinch of salt; do not add more sugar |
| Filling seems loose | Let it stand 5 minutes before eggs; if still very loose, hold back 1 to 2 Tbsp cream next time |
| Streusel is sandy | Add melted butter 1 tsp at a time until small damp clumps form |
| Streusel browns too fast | Tent loosely with foil until the filling is fully hot |
| Marshmallows are pale | Broil briefly rather than extending the casserole bake |
| Marshmallows are scorching | Pull immediately; residual heat will continue to toast them |
| Candied pecans are burning | They went under the broiler; reserve the finishing pecans until after broiling |

## Chef's Notes

The candied cinnamon pecans are not treated like raw pecans. Their coating already supplies sugar and cinnamon, so the streusel uses restrained additional spice and the reserved finishing pecans stay out of the broiler.

The black pepper is intentional. This is not a token holiday-recipe pinch: the casserole should finish with a warm, savory pepper note that makes the roasted sweet potato, brown butter, and salt taste more vivid.

Roasting whole sweet potatoes is preferred to boiling because it avoids adding water and keeps the filling concentrated. The wire rack is particularly useful in the non-convection oven because it lets hot air reach the underside while the lined pan catches sticky drips.

<details>
<summary>Source / Research Notes</summary>

Research completed before build on 2026-10-07.

- **Southern Living:** Southern holiday versions commonly use smooth sweet potatoes with pecan-brown-sugar crunch; marshmallow-topped versions remain a recognizable Thanksgiving tradition. Texture contrast is a major part of the dish's identity.
- **Serious Eats:** Sweet potatoes develop deeper natural sweetness when their internal starches spend time in the amylase-active temperature range; whole roasting supports concentrated flavor and avoids waterlogging.
- **Bon Appétit:** Roasting rather than boiling is a strong casserole technique because it intensifies sweet-potato flavor and keeps the mash from becoming watery.
- **Food Network Kitchen / Virginia Willis:** Strong classic patterns include whole-baked potatoes, eggs, butter/dairy, brown sugar, warm spice, salt, black pepper, and pecan topping.
- **Bobby Flay direction:** Sweet potatoes pair well with dark sugar/molasses character, pecans, salt, black pepper, and restrained savory heat; this supports the house decision to make pepper a real counterweight.
- **Amy & Don house variation:** Use cinnamon candied pecans, brown butter, maple, an assertive 1 tsp black-pepper baseline, marshmallow crown, and post-broil pecan finish.

</details>
