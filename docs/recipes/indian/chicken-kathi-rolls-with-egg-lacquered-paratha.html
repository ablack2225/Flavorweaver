Chicken Kathi Rolls with Egg-Lacquered Paratha

Scratch butter-layered paratha griddled with a thin egg lacquer, wrapped around deeply seasoned yogurt-spiced chicken thighs, crisp lettuce, tomato, purple onion, green onion, and a drizzleable cilantro-mint-jalapeño chutney dressing.

Hall of Fame Verdict

Don said these rolls were better than falafel, his longtime favorite. He singled out the chicken flavor, while the scratch paratha proved layered, flexible, and ideal for wrapping.

Kitchen Execution Summary

Hall of Fame Chicken

Very thin chicken-thigh strips marinated with yogurt, ginger-garlic, lime, garam masala, cumin, coriander, chile, turmeric, salt, and pepper, then quickly skillet-seared.

Butter-Layered Paratha

Soft AP-flour dough laminated with melted butter, accordion-pleated, coiled, rested, gently rolled, and griddled until layered and flexible.

Egg Lacquer

A thin egg layer is bonded directly to each paratha before filling so the wrap eats like a true kathi roll.

Chutney Dressing

Cilantro, mint, whole jalapeño, lime, garlic, ginger, cumin, honey, yogurt, and controlled water create a bright, spicy, drizzleable sauce.

Fresh Crunch

Undressed lettuce, seeded tomato, purple onion, and green onion stay crisp and prevent the wrap from becoming heavy or wet.

Optional Sauce Bar

The Hall of Fame service included the house falafel sauces alongside the chutney dressing for customizable richness, heat, and sesame depth.

Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

Chicken marinade: marinate at least 2 hours, preferably overnight.

Paratha dough: mix earlier the same day. Keep it covered during rests; longer rests and staggered breaks do not harm it.

Chutney dressing: make up to 1 day ahead, refrigerate, then stir and re-brighten with lime before serving.

Fresh vegetables: cut close to service and keep undressed.

Optional sauce bar: prepare sauces ahead using Don's Food-Truck Memory Falafel Naan Wraps.

Service Countdown

Morning or night before: marinate chicken.

2 hours before serving: mix paratha dough and rest.

75 minutes before serving: make chutney dressing and any optional house sauces.

60 minutes before serving: laminate and coil parathas; cover and rest again.

35 minutes before serving: cut fresh vegetables and cook chicken; hold chicken loosely covered.

20 minutes before serving: roll and griddle parathas, egg-lacquer each one, fill, roll, and serve hot.

Measured Ingredients by Component

These sections are reference points. The stage bowls and full timeline below carry the execution.

Scratch Butter-Layered Roll Paratha

2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling; 3/4 tsp kosher salt; 1 tsp sugar; 2 Tbsp melted butter, cooled until warm, for dough; 3/4 cup warm water, added gradually with the final 1–2 Tbsp held back unless needed; 3–4 Tbsp melted butter for layering; 2–3 Tbsp melted butter for griddling and egg-lacquering, used as a very thin film.

Yogurt-Spiced Chicken

1 1/2 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs, sliced into very thin bite-size strips; 1/3 cup yogurt; 1 Tbsp ginger-garlic paste; 1 Tbsp lime juice; 1 Tbsp neutral oil; 1 1/2 tsp garam masala; 1 tsp cumin; 1 tsp coriander; 1 tsp paprika or Kashmiri chile powder; 1/2 tsp turmeric; 1/4–1/2 tsp cayenne; 1 tsp kosher salt; 1/2 tsp black pepper; 1–2 tsp neutral oil for skillet; 1 Tbsp butter; 1–2 tsp lime juice for finishing.

Cilantro-Mint-Jalapeño Chutney Dressing

1 packed cup cilantro; 1/4 packed cup mint; 1 whole jalapeño, stem removed with seeds and white ribs retained; 1 large garlic clove; 3/4 tsp ginger; 2 1/2 Tbsp lime juice; 1 tsp honey or sugar; 1/2 tsp kosher salt; 1/2 tsp ground cumin; optional 1/8–1/4 tsp cayenne; 1 Tbsp plain yogurt; 1–3 Tbsp cold water, only as needed for a drizzleable consistency.

Fresh Fillings + Egg Lacquer

4 large eggs; pinch kosher salt; 1 cup finely shredded lettuce; 1 large tomato, watery seed pulp removed and flesh diced; 1/2 medium purple onion, very thinly sliced or finely diced; 2 green onions, thinly sliced; lime wedges for serving.

Optional Hall of Fame Sauce Bar

Serve any or all of the sauces from Don's Food-Truck Memory Falafel Naan Wraps: tzatziki-ish ranch white sauce, fiery red sauce, and lemon tahini sauce. These were offered at the Hall of Fame service but are not required to preserve the core kathi-roll identity.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Lime: divide among the chicken, chutney dressing, and table wedges.

Butter: melt once, then reserve clean measured portions for dough, lamination, griddling, and chicken finish.

Jalapeño: use the whole chile with seeds and white ribs in the chutney dressing for the tested heat level.

Tomato: remove watery seed pulp before dicing so the paratha stays dry enough to wrap.

Eggs: beat immediately before bonding to the parathas.

1 cup lettuce, 1 seeded diced tomato, 1/2 purple onion, and 2 green onions. Keep undressed.

Do Not Forget

This is a scratch paratha recipe, not naan, tortilla, or a shortcut wrap.

Rest the dough twice. Longer covered rests are acceptable and can make rolling easier.

For layers: roll thin, brush lightly with butter, accordion-pleat, gently stretch, coil like a cinnamon roll, and tuck the tail underneath.

Use gentle pressure during final rolling so the layers are not crushed.

Start paratha and egg around 275°F and chicken around 300°F in Amy's hot-running electric skillet.

Cook the first side of each paratha dry; use only a thin butter film after flipping.

Lift chicken from the marinade and leave excess raw marinade in the bowl.

Keep the fresh vegetables undressed. The chutney dressing and optional sauce bar provide moisture and acid.

The egg layer should be thin and bonded to the paratha, not a scrambled egg filling.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Marinate the chicken. Whisk 1/3 cup yogurt, 1 Tbsp ginger-garlic paste, 1 Tbsp lime juice, 1 Tbsp neutral oil, 1 1/2 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander, 1 tsp paprika/Kashmiri chile, 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1/4–1/2 tsp cayenne, 1 tsp kosher salt, and 1/2 tsp black pepper. Toss with 1 1/2 lb very thinly sliced chicken thighs. Marinate 2 hours to overnight.
  2. Mix the butter paratha dough. Whisk 2 cups AP flour, 3/4 tsp kosher salt, and 1 tsp sugar. Add 2 Tbsp warm melted butter and most of the 3/4 cup warm water. Knead 6–8 minutes until smooth, soft, and elastic, adding held-back water only if needed.
  3. Rest the dough. Lightly butter or oil the surface, cover, and rest 45–60 minutes. A longer covered rest is safe when breaks are needed.
  4. Make the chutney dressing. Blend 1 cup cilantro, 1/4 cup mint, 1 whole jalapeño with seeds and ribs, 1 large garlic clove, 3/4 tsp ginger, 2 1/2 Tbsp lime juice, 1 tsp honey/sugar, 1/2 tsp kosher salt, 1/2 tsp cumin, optional 1/8–1/4 tsp cayenne, and 1 Tbsp yogurt. Add cold water 1 Tbsp at a time only as needed for a smooth, drizzleable dressing.
  5. Prepare the fresh fill. Shred 1 cup lettuce. Seed and dice 1 large tomato. Thinly slice or finely dice 1/2 purple onion and slice 2 green onions. Keep undressed.
  6. Divide and rest. Divide the dough into 4 equal balls, cover, and rest 10–15 minutes.
  7. Laminate and coil. Roll one ball into a very thin 9–10 inch circle or oval. Brush with about 1 1/2–2 tsp melted butter. Fold back and forth in 3/4–1 inch accordion pleats, gently stretch the strip, coil it like a cinnamon roll, and tuck the tail underneath. Repeat, cover, and rest 20–30 minutes; up to 60 minutes is acceptable.
  8. Cook the chicken. Heat the electric skillet around 300°F and add 1–2 tsp neutral oil. Lift chicken from the marinade, leaving excess behind. Cook in a single layer, in two batches if needed. Leave the first side undisturbed for 60–90 seconds, then toss and cook another 2–3 minutes, just until cooked through with scattered browned edges. Finish with 1 Tbsp butter and 1–2 tsp lime juice.
  9. Final-roll each paratha. Place one coil spiral-side up, press gently with the palm, then roll from the center outward with light pressure, rotating frequently. Stop around 7–9 inches wide and about 1/8 inch thick.
  10. Griddle the parathas. Set the skillet around 275°F. Cook one paratha dry for 40–60 seconds. Flip, brush with a thin film of melted butter, and cook 30–45 seconds. Flip once more and finish 30–60 seconds until lightly golden, layered, and flexible. Stack covered.
  11. Egg-lacquer. Beat 4 eggs with a pinch of salt. For each paratha, smear the skillet with about 1/2 tsp melted butter, pour in approximately 1 beaten egg, and spread it slightly smaller than the paratha. Immediately lay the paratha over the wet egg, press gently, and cook 45–60 seconds until bonded. Flip for 10–20 seconds to rewarm the plain side.
  12. Fill each roll. Place the paratha egg-side up. Add hot chicken first, drizzle with chutney dressing, then add lettuce, tomato, purple onion, and green onion. Add optional falafel sauces at the table rather than overloading the wrap. Finish with lime.
  13. Roll and serve. Fold the bottom slightly over the filling, tuck the sides if desired, and roll firmly. Set seam-side down and serve immediately.

Electric Skillet Notes

Chicken: start around 300°F with a thin film of neutral oil. Very thin strips cook in approximately 3–4 minutes total.

Paratha: start around 275°F. Cook the first side dry and butter only after flipping.

Egg lacquer: use 250–275°F if the egg browns before it bonds.

Between batches: wipe away dark butter solids so later parathas do not taste burnt.

Final Taste Target

The finished roll should be hot, buttery, layered, juicy, deeply spiced, herb-bright, and fresh: flexible flaky paratha, thin savory egg, intensely seasoned chicken, crisp undressed vegetables, vivid jalapeño-cilantro-mint dressing, and optional sauce-bar contrast. It must taste like a complete Hall of Fame kathi roll, never a generic chicken wrap.

Chef's Note

The chicken flavor is canon-locked. Melted butter successfully replaced ghee, while the fresh vegetables proved better for this build than a dressed onion crunch because the chutney dressing and sauce bar already provide moisture and acid. Keep dough and coils covered during rests; the paratha tolerates breaks and benefits from relaxation.
