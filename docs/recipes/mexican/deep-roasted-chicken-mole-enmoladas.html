Deep-Roasted Chicken Mole Enmoladas

Tender shredded chicken thighs wrapped in homemade corn tortillas and coated with a deeply roasted mole built from Doña María concentrate, guajillo, charred tomato, onion, garlic, toasted sesame and nuts, and a restrained amount of 60% bittersweet chocolate. These are assembled near service—not baked into a casserole.

Yield7 enmoladas
Total Time1 hr 35 min–1 hr 50 min
DifficultyProject Recipe
Best ServiceDip, fill, and serve
Chicken ThighsDoña MaríaGuajillo60% BittersweetFully Measured

Kitchen Execution Summary

Chicken and Broth

Gently simmer about 1 pound boneless skinless chicken thighs with onion, garlic, bay, salt, and pepper. Pull at 180–190°F for juicy shredding and reserve the broth.

Roasted Mole Foundation

Toast guajillo, char tomato, onion, and garlic, toast sesame, nuts, and bread, then blend and strain before frying the puree until dark and concentrated.

Mole Finish

Fry in Doña María paste, add broth gradually, simmer slowly, and add exactly 1/2 ounce of 60% bittersweet chocolate during the final 10–15 minutes.

Service

Oil-condition tortillas, dip in hot mole, fill with mole-coated chicken, roll, sauce, garnish, and serve immediately. Do not bake.

Before Cooking / Advance Prep

Best make-ahead move: The mole may be made one day ahead. Reheat gently and restore the dipping consistency with warm chicken broth.

Near service only: Condition, dip, and fill tortillas only after the garnishes, platter, chicken, and hot mole are ready.

Service Countdown

90 minutes before serving: Begin the chicken; toast, roast, and stage the mole foundation while it simmers.

60 minutes before serving: Blend, strain, and fry the roasted puree; add Doña María and broth.

30 minutes before serving: Add chocolate during the final simmer, balance the mole, and coat the shredded chicken.

At service: Condition tortillas, dip, fill, roll, sauce, garnish, and carry directly to the table.

Full Enmolada Ingredient Roll-Up

Measured Ingredients by Component

These sections are reference. The complete one-pass cooking flow appears below.

Chicken and Broth

1 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs, 3 average or 4 small; 3 cups water; 1/4 medium onion; 2 lightly crushed garlic cloves; 1 bay leaf; 1/2 tsp fine salt; 1/2 tsp black peppercorns or 1/4 tsp ground pepper.

Roasted Mole Foundation

1 dried guajillo, stemmed and seeded; 1 medium Roma tomato, halved; 1/4 medium onion, thick wedges; 2 skin-on garlic cloves; 1 Tbsp sesame seeds; 2 Tbsp roasted unsalted peanuts or almonds; 1/2 slice firm bread, toasted dark, or 3 saltines; 1/2 chipotle in adobo; 1/4 tsp cinnamon; tiny pinch ground clove; 1 cup warm reserved chicken broth.

Mole Concentrate and Finish

1 Tbsp neutral oil; 4 oz Doña María mole paste; 1–1 1/2 cups warm reserved chicken broth; 1/2 oz / 14 g Ghirardelli 60% bittersweet chips, about 1 1/2 level Tbsp; fine salt; up to 2 tsp honey or brown sugar only if needed; up to 1/2 tsp cider vinegar only if needed.

Filling, Tortillas, and Garnish

1 3/4 cups shredded chicken; 1/2 cup finished mole; 1–2 Tbsp broth only if needed; 7 thawed homemade corn tortillas; 2–3 Tbsp neutral oil; 1–1 1/2 cups hot mole for dipping and topping; 1/4 cup white onion; 1 Tbsp toasted sesame; 1/4 cup crema or thinned sour cream; 2 Tbsp cilantro.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Chicken broth: Reserve at least 2 1/2 cups. Use 1 cup in the blender, 1–1 1/2 cups to loosen the mole, and keep a small amount for consistency and filling corrections.

Sesame: Toast 1 Tbsp for the mole and a separate 1 Tbsp for garnish so the table finish is not accidentally blended.

Chicken: Measure 1 3/4 cups after shredding and reserve any extra before adding mole.

Mole: Protect 1/2 cup for the chicken and 1–1 1/2 cups for tortilla dipping and final saucing.

Stage-Based Prep Bowls

Mole Workflow

Bowl 1 — Roast and Toast

1 halved Roma tomato, 1/4 onion in wedges, 2 skin-on garlic cloves, 1 Tbsp sesame, 2 Tbsp peanuts or almonds, and 1/2 slice bread.

Bowl 2 — Blender Cup

Softened guajillo, all roasted and toasted ingredients, 1/2 chipotle, 1/4 tsp cinnamon, tiny pinch clove, and 1 cup warm broth.

Bowl 3 — Mole Finish

1 Tbsp oil, 4 oz Doña María paste, 1–1 1/2 cups broth, 1/2 oz 60% chocolate, salt, and optional restrained sweet-acid corrections.

Assembly Workflow

Bowl 1 — Chicken Filling

1 3/4 cups shredded chicken, 1/2 cup finished mole, and 1–2 Tbsp broth only if needed.

Bowl 2 — Tortilla Station

7 thawed homemade corn tortillas, 2–3 Tbsp neutral oil, tongs, hot mole, and a warm platter.

Bowl 3 — Table Finish

1/4 cup white onion, 1 Tbsp toasted sesame, 1/4 cup crema or thinned sour cream, 2 Tbsp cilantro, and optional lime wedges.

Do Not Forget

Toast guajillo and sesame gently. Burnt chile or sesame cannot be rescued.

Fry the blended puree until darkened and concentrated before adding the jarred mole paste.

Add broth gradually and keep the mole at a lazy simmer, scraping often.

Add exactly 1/2 ounce / 14 grams of 60% bittersweet chocolate for the first test. It should disappear into the chile.

Salt before adding sweetness. Sugar cannot replace correct seasoning.

Do not bake the assembled enmoladas.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Cook the chicken gently. Combine 1 lb chicken thighs, 3 cups water, 1/4 onion, 2 crushed garlic cloves, 1 bay leaf, 1/2 tsp salt, and 1/2 tsp peppercorns. Bring only to a gentle simmer, partially cover, and cook 22–30 minutes, until the thighs reach about 180–190°F and pull apart easily.
  2. Rest, shred, and reserve broth. Rest the chicken 10 minutes. Strain and keep at least 2 1/2 cups broth warm. Shred the chicken and measure 1 3/4 cups for the filling.
  3. Toast and soak the guajillo. Toast 1 stemmed and seeded guajillo for 10–15 seconds per side, only until fragrant. Cover with very hot water for 15 minutes, then drain.
  4. Roast the fresh base. Dry-roast 1 halved Roma tomato, 1/4 onion in wedges, and 2 skin-on garlic cloves for 8–12 minutes, until softened with deep blistered spots. Peel the garlic.
  5. Toast sesame, nuts, and bread. Separately toast 1 Tbsp sesame until pale golden, 2 Tbsp peanuts or almonds until aromatic, and 1/2 slice bread until deeply browned at the edges.
  6. Blend and strain. Blend the softened guajillo, all roasted produce, toasted sesame, nuts, and bread, 1/2 chipotle, 1/4 tsp cinnamon, tiny pinch clove, and 1 cup warm broth for 1–2 full minutes. Press through a medium-mesh sieve.
  7. Fry the roasted puree. Heat 1 Tbsp neutral oil over medium. Carefully add the strained puree and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring and scraping, until darkened, thick, and free of raw tomato aroma.
  8. Add the mole concentrate. Reduce to medium-low. Add 4 oz Doña María paste and fry it into the puree for 2–3 minutes.
  9. Loosen and simmer. Whisk in 1 cup warm broth in four additions, then add up to another 1/2 cup as needed. Partially cover and simmer gently for 25–30 minutes, stirring every few minutes.
  10. Add chocolate late. During the final 10–15 minutes, whisk in 1/2 oz / 14 g Ghirardelli 60% bittersweet chips. Continue the gentle simmer until the chocolate is fully integrated.
  11. Balance correctly. Salt until the roasted chile and nuts become clear. Only then add 1 tsp honey or brown sugar if the mole has a hard bitter edge. Retaste before adding more. Add cider vinegar only by drops, up to 1/2 tsp, if the fully seasoned sauce still tastes heavy or flat.
  12. Season the chicken. Fold 1/2 cup finished mole into 1 3/4 cups shredded chicken. Add 1–2 Tbsp broth only if needed. Keep warm and covered.
  13. Stage the complete service. Put the hot mole, chicken, platter, white onion, toasted sesame, crema, and cilantro within reach before conditioning the tortillas.
  14. Condition the tortillas. Heat a shallow skillet over medium with a thin layer from the 2–3 Tbsp neutral oil. Warm each tortilla 5–8 seconds per side until supple but not crisp. Stack under a towel.
  15. Dip, fill, and roll. Dip one tortilla briefly into the hot mole, add 1/4 cup mole-coated chicken, roll or fold, and place seam-side down on the warm platter. Repeat for all seven.
  16. Sauce and serve. Spoon 1–1 1/2 cups hot mole over the enmoladas. Finish with white onion, toasted sesame, a restrained crema drizzle, and cilantro. Serve immediately.

Final Taste Target

The mole should open with roasted guajillo and dark toasted aromatics, expand into sesame, nuts, chipotle, and warm spice, then finish with a smooth bittersweet depth that does not announce chocolate. The chicken should remain juicy and clearly seasoned. Onion, sesame, crema, and cilantro should provide contrast without covering the mole.

Chef's Notes

Top-tier shortcut: Doña María is the foundation, not the finished sauce. Roasting, toasting, straining, frying, broth building, chocolate restraint, and simmering create the final mole.

Chocolate control: More is not automatically better. The correct amount makes the chile and nuts taste deeper without turning the sauce into chocolate mole by obvious flavor.

Protected identity: Cheddar, pepper jack, mozzarella, Velveeta Queso Blanco, Hot Rotel, and chorizo are intentionally excluded from these enmoladas.
