# Golden Jeera Basmati

> Fluffy, ivory basmati infused with toasted cumin, cardamom, bay and ghee, cooked in savory chicken broth and finished with a little extra ghee.

## Recipe Dashboard

| Status | Total Time | Servings / Yield | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flavorweaver Test Recipe | About 1 hour (cooker dependent) | 4 / about 4 cups | Easy |

## Kitchen Execution Summary

Rinse and drain rice, bloom whole spices in ghee, coat the rice, then cook in chicken broth in the rice cooker. Rest before fluffing and finish with ghee.

## Ingredient List

- 1½ cups Basmati rice
- 2 cups Regular chicken broth
- 1½ Tbsp Ghee
- 1¼ tsp Whole cumin seeds
- 2 Green cardamom pods
- 1 small Bay leaf
- ½ tsp Lemon juice
- 1 Tbsp Fresh cilantro

## Prep Notes

Use the same measuring cup for rice and broth; use your established rice-cooker ratio for the rice and soaking method. Soak aged basmati if appropriate. No extra salt initially.

## Stage-Based Prep Bowls

### Bowl 1 — Rice

1½ cups basmati rice; rinse, soak 15–20 minutes if aged, then drain thoroughly.

### Bowl 2 — Bloom

1 Tbsp ghee, 1¼ tsp whole cumin seeds, 2 lightly cracked cardamom pods, 1 small bay leaf.

### Bowl 3 — Cooker

2 cups regular chicken broth, or your usual rice-cooker liquid ratio for 1½ cups rice.

### Bowl 4 — Finish

½ Tbsp ghee; optional ½ tsp lemon juice and 1 Tbsp chopped cilantro. No added salt until tasting.

## Do Not Forget

- Use whole cumin seeds and bloom gently; burned cumin tastes bitter.
- Drain soaked rice thoroughly; adjust broth to the rice cooker and rice type rather than forcing a fixed ratio.
- Regular broth supplies salt; taste before adding any.
- Rest 10 minutes before fluffing; remove whole cardamom and bay.

## Instructions

1. **Rinse and drain.** Rinse 1½ cups basmati in cool water until mostly clear. If using aged basmati, soak 15–20 minutes, then drain well. For unaged rice or rice labeled no-soak, skip soaking.

2. **Bloom the spices.** Warm 1 Tbsp ghee over medium-low heat. Add 1¼ tsp whole cumin seeds, 2 lightly cracked green cardamom pods and 1 small bay leaf. Stir for 30–45 seconds until fragrant; do not scorch.

3. **Coat the rice.** Add the drained rice and gently stir for 1–2 minutes to coat the grains. Do not brown or break the rice.

4. **Cook.** Transfer rice and all aromatics to the rice cooker. Add 2 cups regular chicken broth, or use the familiar cooker ratio for this rice and soaking method. Measure rice and broth with the same cup. No additional salt at this stage. Run the normal white rice setting.

5. **Rest and finish.** When the cooker finishes, leave the lid closed 10 minutes. Remove bay leaf and cardamom pods. Add the remaining ½ Tbsp ghee and gently fluff with a fork. Taste for salt; add optional ½ tsp lemon juice and 1 Tbsp chopped cilantro if desired.

6. **Serve.** Serve warm beside Shahi Korma Malia Kofta, Palak Paneer, or your favorite Indian curry.

## Final Taste Target

Long, separate, tender grains with nutty ghee, warm cumin, subtle cardamom and a clean savory broth finish.

## Troubleshooting / Rescue Notes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too salty | Use part water in place of broth next time; skip added salt. |
| Too soft | Reduce liquid slightly next batch or skip soaking. |
| Underdone | Add a splash of hot water and cook gently until tender. |
| Bitter spices | Bloom on medium-low heat; discard scorched spices and restart. |

## Chef's Notes

Keep the rice pale and aromatic, not heavily spiced; it should support creamy korma and spinach curry.

If the rice is too soft, reduce broth slightly next time or skip soaking. If underdone, add a small splash of hot water and cook gently until tender.

For leftovers, cool promptly, refrigerate within 2 hours and reheat until steaming hot.
