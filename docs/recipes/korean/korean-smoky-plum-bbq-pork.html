Korean Smoky-Plum BBQ Pork Hoagies

Soft homemade hoagie rolls filled with overnight-marinated pork butt that is hard-seared, gently braised until pull-apart tender, rested in its juices, coarse-pulled, and lacquered with a smoky fresh-plum gochujang glaze. Spicy mayo and cold sesame pickle crunch complete the sandwich.

Active PrepAbout 1 hr 15 min
Pork Cook3½-4 hr
Yield4 loaded hoagies
Marinate8-24 hr
pull-apart porkfresh plum glazespicy mayopickle crunchhomemade hoagies

Start Here: Complete Kitchen Execution Order

1. Start the Frozen Rolls

Thaw and proof the four shaped hoagie rolls under lightly oiled plastic. They are ready when fully thawed, visibly puffy, and a fingertip dent returns slowly.

2. Sear and Braise

Drain and lightly dry the overnight-marinated pork. Hard-sear in batches, build the onion braise, cover tightly, and cook at 315°F.

3. Build the Cold Components

During the braise, make the two-plum barbecue glaze, spicy gochujang mayo, and quick-pickled onion-carrot-jalapeño.

4. Cook to Pull-Apart

Do not stop at ordinary doneness. Continue until a fork enters with almost no resistance and twisting begins to separate the meat.

5. Rest, Bake, Pull

Rest pork covered in its liquid 20-30 minutes. Bake the fully proofed rolls at 350°F. Coarse-pull the rested pork.

6. Restore, Lacquer, Build

Fold reduced pork liquid into the meat, add plum glaze, broil for sticky edges, then build on butter-toasted rolls with mayo and cold crunch.

Non-Negotiable Texture Standard

This is not an intact-chunk recipe anymore. A hoagie needs pork that yields easily with each bite.

Cook until pull-apart: expect roughly 2½-3¼ hours at 315°F after searing, but tenderness—not the clock—decides.

Rest in liquid: leave the tender pork covered in its braising liquid 20-30 minutes.

Coarse-pull: make short substantial shreds and small tender pieces, not dry hairlike strands.

Restore moisture first: fold in reduced braising liquid before adding plum glaze.

Char briefly: broil only long enough to create sticky edges while the interior remains juicy.

Measured Ingredients by Component

Pork and Overnight Marinade

2 lb pork butt, cut into 1½- to 2-inch chunks; 2 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 2 Tbsp gochujang; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar; 1 Tbsp honey; ⅓ cup grated sweet onion; 6 grated garlic cloves; 1 Tbsp grated ginger; whites/light greens from 2 green onions; 1 tsp gochugaru; ½ tsp black pepper; ½ tsp MSG; 1 tsp toasted sesame oil.

Savory Braise

1 Tbsp oil; 1 cup sliced sweet onion; ¾ cup lower-sodium chicken broth; ½ cup water; 1 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 1 Tbsp gochujang; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar.

Charred Fresh-Plum BBQ Glaze

1½ lb fresh plums, skins on, halved and pitted; 2 Tbsp gochujang; 3 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; ¼ cup rice vinegar; 3 Tbsp honey; up to 1 Tbsp brown sugar; 1 Tbsp tomato paste; 1 Tbsp grated ginger; 4 grated garlic cloves; 1 tsp smoked paprika; 1 tsp gochugaru; ½ cup water; 1 tsp toasted sesame oil off heat.

Spicy Gochujang Mayo

½ cup mayonnaise; 1½ Tbsp gochujang; 2 tsp lime juice; 1 tsp rice vinegar; 1 tsp honey; ¼ tsp garlic powder; ¼ tsp toasted sesame oil; optional ¼ tsp gochugaru.

Quick Pickle and Sesame Crunch

½ small red onion, paper-thin; 1 carrot, fine matchsticks; ½-1 jalapeño, thinly sliced; ⅓ cup rice vinegar; ¼ cup hot water; 1 Tbsp honey or sugar; ¾ tsp fine salt.

At service add 1 packed cup shredded green cabbage, 1 sliced green onion, 1 tsp sesame seeds, and ½ tsp toasted sesame oil.

Hoagie Rolls and Assembly

4 frozen shaped unproofed hoagie rolls, about 135-150 g each; 2 Tbsp melted butter; about 5-6 oz finished pork per roll; spicy mayo; sesame pickle crunch; clean plum sauce; scallion tops; sesame seeds; optional lime.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Start the frozen rolls. Place the four frozen shaped rolls on parchment with room between them. Cover with lightly oiled plastic or enclose the tray in a food-safe bag. Let thaw and proof until visibly puffy and a fingertip dent returns slowly. This may take 3-5 hours. If ready early, refrigerate covered for up to about 1 hour.
  2. Temper and dry the pork. Remove the marinated pork 30-40 minutes before searing. Let excess marinade drip away, lightly pat the surfaces dry, and discard the raw marinade. Heat oven to 315°F.
  3. Sear in batches. Heat a Dutch oven over medium-high to high heat with enough oil to film the bottom. Brown pork deeply in 2-3 uncrowded batches. Transfer to a tray without cooking the centers through.
  4. Build the braise. Cook sliced onion in the rendered pork fat about 5 minutes. Add broth, water, soy, gochujang, and vinegar, scraping up all fond.
  5. Braise for genuine tenderness. Return pork and juices. Liquid should reach one-third to halfway up the chunks. Cover tightly and braise at 315°F. Begin checking around 2 hours 15 minutes, then every 20 minutes. Pork is ready when a fork enters with almost no resistance and twisting separates the meat; it will commonly read about 198-205°F.
  6. Char half the plums. Broil half the pitted plums cut-side up 6-10 minutes until blistered with dark caramelized spots.
  7. Make the plum glaze. Simmer all plums with gochujang, soy, vinegar, honey, optional brown sugar, tomato paste, ginger, garlic, smoked paprika, gochugaru, and water for 20-30 minutes until collapsed.
  8. Blend, strain, and reduce. Blend smooth, strain through fine mesh, and reduce gently to about 1¾-2 cups. Remove from heat and stir in sesame oil. Correct jamminess with vinegar 1 tsp at a time.
  9. Make the spicy mayo. Whisk all mayo ingredients smooth and chill at least 30 minutes.
  10. Quick-pickle the vegetables. Pour the hot water-vinegar-honey-salt brine over onion, carrot, and jalapeño. Rest at least 30 minutes, stirring once.
  11. Rest the pork. When tender, remove the Dutch oven from the oven but leave the pork tightly covered in its liquid for 20-30 minutes. Raise oven to 350°F.
  12. Bake the proofed rolls. Bake 18-22 minutes until golden and 190-195°F internally. Brush warm tops lightly with butter. Cool at least 20 minutes before splitting.
  13. Coarse-pull the pork. Lift pork to a wide bowl or tray and break into substantial short shreds and small chunks. Cover loosely.
  14. Reduce the pork liquid. Strain the braising liquid, skim excess fat, and boil to about ⅓ cup.
  15. Restore moisture. Fold ¼ cup reduced braising liquid into the pork, followed by about ½ cup clean plum glaze. Reserve at least ½-¾ cup clean sauce for the table.
  16. Build the broiler lacquer. Whisk another ¼ cup clean glaze with 1-2 Tbsp reduced pork liquid.
  17. Lacquer and char. Spread pork loosely on a foil-lined broiler pan. Add half the lacquer and broil 2-4 minutes. Turn sections gently, apply the remaining lacquer, and broil another 1-3 minutes until sticky dark edges appear.
  18. Finish the crunch. Drain the pickled vegetables extremely well. Toss about ¾ cup drained pickles with cabbage, green onion, sesame seeds, and sesame oil. Add no more than 1 tsp pickle liquid if needed.
  19. Butter-toast the rolls. Split without cutting through the hinge. Brush cut faces lightly with butter and toast cut-side down in a hot skillet until golden.
  20. Build. Spread spicy mayo over both toasted faces. Add 5-6 oz hot lacquered pork, only a small spoonful of clean plum sauce if needed, then sesame pickle crunch, scallion tops, and sesame seeds. Serve immediately.

Service Countdown

5 hours before: begin covered thaw/proof of rolls and temper pork.

4½ hours before: sear pork, build braise, and place covered in the 315°F oven.

During braise: make plum glaze, spicy mayo, and quick pickles.

40 minutes before: rest tender pork covered in liquid; raise oven to 350°F.

35 minutes before: bake proofed rolls.

20 minutes before: coarse-pull pork, reduce liquid, restore moisture, and build lacquer.

10 minutes before: broil pork, finish crunch, split and toast rolls.

Service: mayo → pork → optional clean glaze → crunch → scallion and sesame.

Troubleshooting

Pork still tough: re-cover and continue braising in 20-minute increments. Ordinary safe temperature is not the tenderness endpoint.

Pork dry: fold in warm reduced braising liquid 1 Tbsp at a time before adding more glaze.

Glaze jammy: add rice vinegar 1 tsp at a time, then a little water or soy if needed.

Crunch wet: drain or lightly squeeze the pickles and add more dry cabbage.

Rolls ready early: refrigerate covered up to about 1 hour, then let stand while pork finishes.

Sandwich too sweet: add more pickle crunch or lime; do not add more plum glaze.

Chef's Note

The finished hoagie relies on three contrasting textures and temperatures: hot juicy-charred pork, cold sharp cabbage-pickle crunch, and warm butter-toasted bread. Spicy mayo goes on both cut faces so every bite is seasoned and the crumb is protected from meat juices.

The defining house method is marinate → hard sear → covered braise until pull-apart → rest in liquid → coarse-pull → restore juices → plum lacquer → char → build.
