Korean Smoky-Plum BBQ Pork

Pork-butt chunks marinated with gochujang, soy, ginger, garlic, and scallion; hard-seared, gently braised until tender, then lacquered under high heat with a polished fresh-plum barbecue glaze that is smoky, fermented, sweet-hot, and bright rather than jammy.

Prep50 min
CookAbout 3 hr
Serves4-6
Marinate8-24 hr
pork buttfresh plumgochujangsear-braise-lacquerfreezer friendly

Kitchen Execution Summary

Overnight Foundation

The pork receives a restrained but assertive gochujang-soy marinade with grated onion, garlic, ginger, scallion, chile, MSG, honey, vinegar, and a small amount of sesame oil.

Tenderness Method

Hard searing creates fond before the pork-butt chunks are gently braised at 315°F until fork-tender but still intact.

Two-Plum Glaze

Half the plums are broiled for caramelized depth; half remain uncharred for fresh acidity. The sauce is blended, strained, and reduced for a polished lacquer.

Final Restaurant Finish

Clean plum glaze is reserved first. A separate portion receives reduced pork braising liquor, coats the tender meat, and is broiled into sticky charred edges.

Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

Marinate the pork — Coat the 2 lb pork-butt chunks and refrigerate airtight for 8-24 hours.

Optional sauce head start — The complete strained plum glaze may be made 2-3 days ahead. Refrigerate it, then warm gently before dividing it for service and lacquering.

Make-ahead braise — The pork may be braised one day early and chilled in its cooking liquid. Rewarm gently before reducing the liquid, lacquering, and broiling.

Service Countdown

Day before: marinate the pork; optionally prepare the plum glaze.

3 hours before serving: temper and dry the pork, preheat the oven, char the plums if the glaze is not already made, and begin searing.

45 minutes before serving: check pork tenderness; stage the broiler pan, green onions, sesame seeds, pickles, rice, buns, or lettuce wraps.

15 minutes before serving: reduce the braising liquid, divide the clean plum sauce, build the lacquer, and broil the pork.

At service: finish with green onions and sesame seeds; serve clean plum sauce separately.

Full Recipe Ingredient Roll-Up

Measured Ingredients by Component

Use these reference sections for measuring. Follow the stage-based bowls and full timeline for execution.

Pork and Overnight Marinade

2 lb pork butt, cut into 1 1/2- to 2-inch chunks; 2 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 2 Tbsp gochujang; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar; 1 Tbsp honey; 1/3 cup finely grated sweet onion; 6 grated garlic cloves; 1 Tbsp grated fresh ginger; whites and light greens from 2 sliced green onions; 1 tsp gochugaru or 1/2 tsp crushed red pepper; 1/2 tsp black pepper; 1/2 tsp MSG; 1 tsp toasted sesame oil.

Savory Braise

1 Tbsp olive oil; 1/2 large sweet onion, about 1 cup sliced; 3/4 cup chicken broth; 1/2 cup water, plus more only if needed; 1 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 1 Tbsp gochujang; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar.

Charred Fresh-Plum BBQ Glaze

1 1/2 lb fresh plums, pitted and divided in half; 2 Tbsp gochujang; 3 Tbsp less-sodium soy sauce; 1/4 cup rice vinegar; 3 Tbsp honey; up to 1 Tbsp light brown sugar; 1 Tbsp tomato paste; 1 Tbsp grated ginger; 4 grated garlic cloves; 1 tsp smoked paprika; 1 tsp gochugaru; 1/2 cup water; 1 tsp toasted sesame oil after cooking.

Final Lacquer and Finish

3/4 cup clean plum glaze; up to 1/4 cup reduced braising liquid; 1-2 tsp rice vinegar or lime juice only if needed; reserved green onion tops; 1 Tbsp toasted sesame seeds; about 1 cup remaining clean plum sauce for table service or freezing.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Green onions: use whites and light-green portions in the marinade; reserve the dark tops for service.

Gochujang: measure 2 Tbsp for the marinade, 1 Tbsp for the braise, and 2 Tbsp for the plum glaze.

Rice vinegar: measure 1 Tbsp for the marinade, 1 Tbsp for the braise, and 1/4 cup for the glaze; keep a little extra available for the final balance.

Fresh ginger and garlic: the marinade receives 1 Tbsp ginger and 6 garlic cloves; the glaze receives 1 Tbsp ginger and 4 garlic cloves.

Plum sauce: divide it before reduced braising liquid touches it. The table sauce must remain clean.

Stage-Based Prep Bowls

Overnight and Braise Setup

Bag 1 — Pork Marinade

2 lb pork plus all measured marinade ingredients. Refrigerate 8-24 hours.

Bowl 2 — Onion Base

1 cup sliced sweet onion for the Dutch oven after the pork sears.

Bowl 3 — Braising Liquid

3/4 cup broth, 1/2 cup water, 1 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 Tbsp gochujang, 1 Tbsp rice vinegar.

Plum Sauce and Final Lacquer

Tray 1 — Char Plums

Half of the pitted plums, cut side up, ready for the broiler.

Bowl 2 — Sauce Base

Remaining plums plus gochujang, soy, vinegar, honey, brown sugar, tomato paste, ginger, garlic, smoked paprika, gochugaru, and water.

Bowl 3 — Table Finish

Green onion tops, toasted sesame seeds, optional final vinegar or lime, clean sauce container, and lacquer bowl.

Do Not Forget

Discard the raw pork marinade. Do not turn it into serving sauce.

Sear in uncrowded batches. Pale steamed pork cannot create the same braising depth.

The pork must become tender before the sugar-rich plum glaze is added.

Reserve about 1 cup clean plum sauce before adding reduced pork liquid to the lacquer portion.

Broil in thin coats and remain at the oven; the glaze can burn quickly.

Taste before serving and correct acid before adding more sugar.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Marinate the pork. Whisk 2 Tbsp soy sauce, 2 Tbsp gochujang, 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp honey, 1/3 cup grated onion, 6 garlic cloves, 1 Tbsp ginger, sliced whites/light greens from 2 green onions, 1 tsp gochugaru, 1/2 tsp black pepper, 1/2 tsp MSG, and 1 tsp sesame oil. Coat 2 lb pork-butt chunks, cover, and refrigerate 8-24 hours.
  2. Temper, drain, and dry. Pull the pork 30-40 minutes before cooking. Let excess marinade drip away, pat the surfaces lightly dry, discard the raw marinade, and heat the oven to 315°F.
  3. Char half the plums. Broil half of the 1 1/2 lb pitted plums, cut side up, until blistered with dark caramelized spots, about 6-10 minutes.
  4. Build the plum sauce. Simmer all plums with 2 Tbsp gochujang, 3 Tbsp soy sauce, 1/4 cup rice vinegar, 3 Tbsp honey, up to 1 Tbsp brown sugar, 1 Tbsp tomato paste, 1 Tbsp ginger, 4 garlic cloves, 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp gochugaru, and 1/2 cup water for 20-30 minutes.
  5. Blend, strain, and reduce. Blend completely smooth, strain through a fine mesh, and reduce gently to about 1 3/4-2 cups. Remove from heat and stir in 1 tsp toasted sesame oil. Correct jamminess with vinegar before adding more sweetness.
  6. Sear the pork. Heat a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high to high heat with up to 1 Tbsp olive oil. Brown the pork deeply in 2-3 uncrowded batches; transfer to a tray.
  7. Build the braise. Cook 1 cup sliced sweet onion in the rendered fat until softened and browned at the edges. Add 3/4 cup chicken broth, 1/2 cup water, 1 Tbsp soy sauce, 1 Tbsp gochujang, and 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, scraping up every browned bit.
  8. Braise until tender. Return the pork and juices. Liquid should reach roughly one-third up the chunks. Cover and braise at 315°F. Check after 1 hour 45 minutes, then every 20-30 minutes. Total time is usually 2-2 1/2 hours. Stop when a fork enters easily but the chunks still hold shape.
  9. Reduce the pork liquid. Move the pork carefully to a broiler-safe sheet. Strain the braising liquid, skim excess fat, and boil it down to about 1/3 cup.
  10. Divide sauce and build lacquer. Seal about 1 cup clean plum glaze for serving. Mix 3/4 cup clean glaze with 2 Tbsp reduced braising liquid. Taste; add more reduction only as needed, up to 1/4 cup total.
  11. Lacquer and char. Coat the pork without creating puddles. Broil 4-8 minutes, turning once and applying a second thin coat, until sticky and lightly charred.
  12. Finish and serve. Add 1-2 tsp rice vinegar or lime only if the finished sauce needs lift. Finish with reserved green onion tops and 1 Tbsp toasted sesame seeds. Serve hot with clean plum sauce.

Serving Paths

Rice Bowl

Serve over rice with quick-pickled carrots and red onion, lettuce, green onions, sesame seeds, and extra clean plum sauce.

Soft-Bun Sandwich

Chop or gently pull the glazed pork and pile into a toasted soft bun with crunchy pickles or slaw and a restrained creamy gochujang drizzle.

Lettuce or Flatbread Wrap

Add crisp lettuce, fresh chile, green onions, pickled vegetables, and just enough plum sauce to carry through the wrap.

Freezer Portion

Freeze the pork with 1-2 Tbsp lacquer per serving and keep extra clean sauce separate.

Troubleshooting and Rescue Notes

Jammy sauce: add rice vinegar 1 tsp at a time, then a small splash of soy or water.

Tough pork: return it to the covered braise and check every 20 minutes.

Falling-apart pork: cool it briefly in a single layer before gently glazing and broiling.

Loose glaze: reduce gently before the sesame oil is added.

Burning glaze: move the pan farther from the element and use thinner coats.

Final Taste Target

The pork should be deeply savory and tender with intact, lacquered chunks and dark caramelized edges. The sauce should read as gochujang, soy, ginger, garlic, pork fond, char, chile, and tart ripe plum in one bite. It must be glossy and fruit-forward without tasting like jam, candy, or generic bottled barbecue sauce.

Chef's Notes

The two-plum technique protects complexity: broiled fruit contributes roasted depth while uncharred fruit keeps the sauce alive. Straining creates the smooth lacquered texture expected from a restaurant-style finishing sauce.

Keep the clean sauce and pork-enriched lacquer separate. That preserves a safe table sauce, lets you control salt precisely, and makes leftovers and freezer portions more versatile.

Reheating: thaw overnight, reheat covered with a splash of water at 325°F or over low skillet heat, then uncover and add fresh sauce near the end. Finish again with fresh green onion and sesame.
