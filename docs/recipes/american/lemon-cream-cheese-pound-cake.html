Lemon Cream Cheese Pound Cake

A magnificently buttery, exponentially lemony cake with the elegant structure of pound cake but a noticeably lighter, more tender fork feel. Fresh lemon sugar, cultured buttermilk, a restrained touch of cream cheese, warm tart syrup, and lemon cream cheese glaze create the Flavorweaver elevation.

TypeDessert / Bundt Cake
TotalAbout 2 hr 45 min including cooling
Yield1 large 12-cup cake / 12 to 16 slices

Kitchen Execution Summary

Intended Crumb

Rich and fine-crumbed like an elegant pound cake, but lighter, tenderer, and easier on the fork. It must never read as dense cream-cheese pound cake.

Fresh Lemon Architecture

A full 1/3 cup zest is rubbed into sugar, then lemon continues through the batter, warm syrup, and glaze. More batter acid is not needed.

Mechanical Lift

The full 4 to 5 minute creaming stage and room-temperature ingredients are essential. The small cream cheese addition improves silkiness without sacrificing lift.

Critical Bake Cue

Start checking at 50 minutes. Pull the cake at a few moist crumbs and approximately 200 to 203°F in the center.

Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

Lemons: set aside about 8 large lemons. Zest before juicing. You need 1/3 cup zest and approximately 1 cup juice across the batter, syrup, and glaze.

Room-temperature ingredients: bring 1 cup butter, both 2-oz cream-cheese portions, 4 whole eggs, and 3/4 cup buttermilk to room temperature.

Pan: use a 12-cup Bundt or tube pan. Flour-containing baker's spray is preferred for intricate designs; otherwise use shortening plus flour in every crease.

Buttermilk backup: add 2 1/4 tsp lemon juice or white vinegar to a measuring cup, add whole milk to reach 3/4 cup, and rest 10 minutes. Do not replace buttermilk with heavy cream.

Service Countdown

60 minutes before mixing: set out butter, cream cheese, eggs, and buttermilk. Eggs can be warmed in a bowl of warm tap water for 5 to 10 minutes.

30 minutes before mixing: zest and juice the lemons, rub zest into sugar, prepare the pan, and measure each component.

15 minutes before mixing: preheat the oven, position the rack in the lower-middle, and prepare the dry and liquid bowls.

50 minutes into baking: begin checking the cake rather than waiting automatically for the maximum time.

10 to 15 minutes after baking: apply syrup to the exposed bottom, invert, and coat the exterior gradually.

After full cooling: glaze and let set 20 to 30 minutes before slicing.

Full Cake Ingredient Roll-Up

Quick-glance ingredient check for the full cake. Exact amounts and division points are repeated in the component sections, prep map, bowls, and cooking timeline.

Tender-Crumb Dry Mix

All-purpose flour, cornstarch, baking powder, baking soda, and fine salt.

Lemon Butter Batter

Granulated sugar, 1/3 cup fresh lemon zest, unsalted butter, a restrained 2 oz cream cheese, 4 whole eggs, buttermilk, lemon juice, and vanilla.

Warm Lemon Syrup

Fresh lemon juice, granulated sugar, and a small pinch of salt.

Lemon Cream Cheese Glaze

Cream cheese, powdered sugar, fresh lemon juice, vanilla, salt, and only enough milk or cream for a thick pourable ribbon.

Measured Ingredients by Component

All primary measurements use ordinary kitchen cups and spoons. Spoon flour into the measuring cup and level it; do not scoop and pack.

Tender-Crumb Dry Mix

2 3/4 cups all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled; 1/4 cup cornstarch; 1 tsp baking powder; 1/2 tsp baking soda; 3/4 tsp fine salt.

Intensely Fragrant Lemon Sugar

2 cups granulated sugar; 1/3 cup finely grated lemon zest, usually from 6 to 8 large lemons. Use only the yellow peel.

Butter-Cream Cheese Base

1 cup / 2 sticks unsalted butter, room temperature but not greasy; 2 oz full-fat block cream cheese, room temperature.

Eggs and Buttermilk Liquid

4 large whole eggs, all room temperature; 3/4 cup cultured buttermilk, room temperature; 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice; 2 tsp vanilla extract. There is no extra yolk.

Warm Lemon Syrup

1/2 cup fresh lemon juice; 1/2 cup granulated sugar; 1 small pinch fine salt.

Lemon Cream Cheese Glaze

2 oz very soft full-fat block cream cheese; 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar; 2 to 3 Tbsp fresh lemon juice; 1/2 tsp vanilla; 1 small pinch fine salt; 1 to 2 Tbsp whole milk or heavy cream only as needed; optional fresh zest.

Cross-Component Prep Map

Lemons: measure 1/3 cup zest first, then reserve 1/4 cup juice for batter, 1/2 cup for syrup, and 2 to 3 Tbsp for glaze.

Cream cheese: divide 4 oz total into 2 oz for batter and 2 oz for glaze.

Sugar: measure 2 cups granulated for batter, 1/2 cup granulated for syrup, and 1 1/2 cups powdered for glaze.

Temperature: butter, batter cream cheese, all 4 eggs, and buttermilk must be room temperature.

Finish order: syrup the warm cake gradually; glaze only after the cake is completely cool.

Stage-Based Prep Bowls

Lemon and Pan Foundation

Bowl 1 — Lemon Sugar

2 cups granulated sugar rubbed with 1/3 cup finely grated lemon zest; rest 15 to 30 minutes.

Pan — Ready to Fill

12-cup Bundt or tube pan coated thoroughly with baker's spray containing flour, or shortening plus flour.

Juice Portions

1/4 cup for batter; 1/2 cup for syrup; 2 to 3 Tbsp for glaze.

Batter and Finish Prep

Bowl 1 — Dry Mix

2 3/4 cups AP flour; 1/4 cup cornstarch; 1 tsp baking powder; 1/2 tsp baking soda; 3/4 tsp fine salt.

Mixer Bowl — Fat Base

1 cup room-temperature butter plus 2 oz room-temperature cream cheese.

Bowl 2 — Eggs

4 large room-temperature whole eggs, added one at a time. No extra egg or yolk.

Bowl 3 — Liquid

3/4 cup room-temperature buttermilk; 1/4 cup lemon juice; 2 tsp vanilla.

Bowl 4 — Syrup

1/2 cup lemon juice; 1/2 cup granulated sugar; pinch fine salt.

Bowl 5 — Glaze

2 oz cream cheese; 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar; lemon juice; vanilla; salt; milk or cream only as needed.

Do Not Forget

The egg requirement is 4 whole large eggs total. There is no fifth egg and no extra yolk.

Room-temperature ingredients and the full 4 to 5 minute creaming stage are essential to the lighter crumb.

Once flour enters the bowl, mix as little as possible and finish by hand.

Do not add sour cream, oil, shortening, heavy cream, or extra cream cheese to the batter during the first revised test.

Keep batter lemon juice at 1/4 cup. Stronger lemon belongs in the zest, syrup, and glaze.

Syrup while warm in gradual coats; glaze only when completely cool.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Temper the ingredients. Set out 1 cup unsalted butter, 2 oz cream cheese for the batter, 4 large whole eggs, and 3/4 cup buttermilk 45 to 60 minutes before mixing. Butter and cream cheese should be pliable but still cool—not shiny or greasy.
  2. Build the lemon sugar. Rub 2 cups granulated sugar with 1/3 cup finely grated lemon zest for 1 to 2 minutes, until pale yellow, slightly damp, and intensely fragrant. Rest 15 to 30 minutes.
  3. Prepare the oven and pan. Position a rack in the lower-middle and preheat to 350°F. For a very dark nonstick Bundt pan, use 325°F. Coat every crease of a 12-cup Bundt or tube pan with flour-containing baker's spray, or shortening plus flour.
  4. Make the dry mix. Whisk 2 3/4 cups all-purpose flour, 1/4 cup cornstarch, 1 tsp baking powder, 1/2 tsp baking soda, and 3/4 tsp fine salt for at least 30 seconds. Sift if possible.
  5. Make the liquid mix. Whisk 3/4 cup room-temperature buttermilk, 1/4 cup lemon juice, and 2 tsp vanilla. Slight curdling is normal.
  6. Smooth the fat base. Beat 1 cup room-temperature butter with 2 oz room-temperature cream cheese on medium for 1 to 2 minutes, scraping once, until completely smooth.
  7. Cream with the lemon sugar. Gradually add the prepared lemon sugar. Beat on medium to medium-high for 4 to 5 full minutes, scraping halfway. The mixture should become visibly paler, fluffier, and increased in volume.
  8. Add all 4 eggs one at a time. On medium-low, add 1 room-temperature egg and beat 20 to 30 seconds before adding the next. Repeat until all 4 eggs are incorporated, scraping after the second and fourth. If curdled, mix in 1 Tbsp prepared dry mix.
  9. Alternate dry and liquid additions. On low, add one third of the dry mix, half the liquid, another third dry, the remaining liquid, then the final dry. Stop while a few flour streaks remain and finish folding gently by hand. Do not beat the finished batter.
  10. Fill the pan. Spoon batter evenly into the prepared pan. Smooth the top, run a thin spatula through once to release large hidden air pockets, and tap the pan only once or twice.
  11. Bake until just done. Bake at 350°F for 50 to 65 minutes; a dark pan at 325°F may need 60 to 75 minutes. Begin checking at 50 minutes. Remove when deeply golden, lightly springy, showing a few moist tester crumbs, and approximately 200 to 203°F in the center.
  12. Make the syrup. During the final 10 minutes, warm 1/2 cup lemon juice, 1/2 cup granulated sugar, and a small pinch fine salt over low heat only until dissolved. Do not boil aggressively.
  13. Syrup the exposed bottom. Cool the cake in the pan for 10 to 15 minutes. Make about 15 shallow holes in the exposed bottom and slowly apply approximately one third of the warm syrup. Pause 2 to 3 minutes.
  14. Invert and finish syruping. Carefully invert the cake. Brush the remaining syrup over the warm top and sides in 2 or 3 light coats, pausing about 2 minutes between coats. Use the full amount while the cake absorbs it, but do not leave syrup pooling at the base.
  15. Cool completely. Let the cake cool for approximately 1 1/2 hours. Do not glaze while warm.
  16. Make the glaze. Beat 2 oz very soft cream cheese smooth. Add 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar, 2 Tbsp lemon juice, 1/2 tsp vanilla, and a small pinch fine salt. Add up to 1 additional Tbsp lemon juice, then 1 to 2 Tbsp milk or cream 1 teaspoon at a time only as needed. The glaze should fall in a thick ribbon.
  17. Glaze and set. Spoon the glaze over the completely cooled cake and add optional zest. Let set 20 to 30 minutes.
  18. Serve and store. Slice with a sharp knife. The cake should be intensely lemony, magnificently buttery, moist, finely textured, and rich without eating dense. Cover tightly at room temperature for up to 3 days.

Final Taste Target

The cake should sit between an elegant lemon butter cake and a classic pound cake: structured enough for beautiful Bundt slices, but lighter and more tender than traditional dense pound cake. Butter leads; lemon blooms immediately and persists through syrup and glaze; cream cheese reads as silkiness and gentle tang rather than cheesecake weight.

Chef's Notes

This elevation protects the architecture that makes the classic Ina-style lemon cake exceptional: properly creamed butter and sugar, abundant fresh zest, cultured buttermilk, controlled batter juice, warm lemon soak, and a bright glaze.

The Flavorweaver version adds only enough cream cheese to improve silkiness and provide a polished glaze. It deliberately removes the former shortening, oil, sour cream, excess cream cheese, fifth egg, and extra yolk that pushed the draft toward a dense cake.

All primary measurements use normal kitchen cups and spoons. Spoon and level the flour so convenient volume measuring does not accidentally produce a dry, heavy cake.

Troubleshooting

IssueLikely Cause / Fix
Dense or tight crumbButter was under-creamed, ingredients were cold, or batter was overmixed after flour. Protect the full creaming time and use room-temperature ingredients.
Greasy or flat cakeButter was too warm or partially melted. Begin with pliable but still cool butter and cream cheese.
Curdled batter after eggsEggs were cold or added too quickly. Mix in 1 Tbsp prepared dry mix, then continue gently.
Gummy centerCake was underbaked, cut warm, or syruped too rapidly. Bake to 200 to 203°F and apply syrup in thin coats.
Dry crumbFlour was packed or cake overbaked. Spoon and level flour and begin checking at 50 minutes.
Weak lemon flavorZest was under-measured or not rubbed into sugar. Use a full 1/3 cup fresh zest; do not add more batter juice.
Glaze disappearsCake was warm or glaze over-thinned. Cool completely and add liquid only 1 teaspoon at a time.
