# Shahi Korma Malia Kofta

> Golden, crisp paneer-potato koftas nestled in a silky light-tan onion-cashew korma gravy, with savory chicken broth, cream, delicate sweetness, cardamom and a lingering serrano warmth.

## Kitchen Execution Summary

Make the gravy first; prepare koftas while it simmers. Hold gravy gently warm, fry koftas at service, and assemble only on the plate.

## Prep Notes

Homemade paneer optional: ½ gallon whole milk, 2–3 Tbsp lemon juice added gradually, cheesecloth and a light press. Cool potato fully. Use a blender, skillet, fine sieve and frying rack. Start Palak Paneer and rice separately.

## Stage-Based Prep Bowls

### Gravy 1 — Onion & Cashew

2 cups sliced sweet onion; ½ cup raw cashews (soaked 20 minutes, drained); 2 Tbsp ghee.

### Gravy 2 — Whole Aromatics

4 cracked green cardamom pods; 1-inch cinnamon stick; 3 cloves; 1 bay leaf.

### Gravy 3 — Fresh Aromatics

5 minced garlic cloves; 1 Tbsp grated ginger; 1 small deseeded, slit serrano (or ½–1 small jalapeño).

### Gravy 4 — Ground Spices

1½ tsp coriander; ½ tsp cumin; ¼ tsp white pepper; tiny pinch nutmeg.

### Gravy 5 — Liquids

1½ cups regular chicken broth, divided into two ¾-cup portions; up to ½ cup water for thinning.

### Gravy 6 — Finish

½ cup cream; 2 Tbsp unsalted butter (1 Tbsp for bloom, 1 Tbsp finish); ½ tsp garam masala; ½ tsp crushed kasuri methi; 1 tsp sugar; up to ¼ tsp fine sea salt to start after tasting; optional few drops lemon.

### Kofta 1 — Dry Base

1½ cups finely grated paneer; ½ cup cooled, finely grated boiled potato.

### Kofta 2 — Seasoning

2½ Tbsp cornstarch (extra 1 Tbsp only if needed); ½ tsp sea salt; ½–¾ tsp garam masala (or ¼ tsp cardamom).

### Kofta 3 — Flavor Mix

1 Tbsp chopped cashews and 1 Tbsp chopped raisins (optional); 1 tsp ginger; 1–2 deseeded chopped green chiles or ½ tsp black pepper; 1 Tbsp coriander leaves.

### Kofta 4 — Fry & Finish

2 cups oil or enough for deep frying; rack; cream, toasted cashews and optional coriander leaves.

## Do Not Forget

- Keep onions light golden and omit tomatoes, red chile powder and turmeric to protect the pale color.
- Regular chicken broth reduces and concentrates; taste before salting, and use water rather than more broth to thin.
- Cool potato and drain paneer well; test-fry before committing the whole batch.
- Remove whole spices and serrano; never boil the cream finish or the fried koftas in the gravy.

## Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

1. **Prep.** Soak ½ cup raw cashews in hot water for 20 minutes and drain. If making paneer, prepare it ahead using ½ gallon whole milk and 2–3 Tbsp lemon juice (add gradually until curds separate); drain and press until dry enough to grate. Boil 1 medium potato, cool completely, and grate ½ cup. Measure 1½ cups paneer.

2. **Light-golden onions.** Heat 2 Tbsp ghee in the skillet at 275–300°F (our skillet runs hot). Cook 2 cups sliced sweet onion for 12–15 minutes, stirring, until soft and light golden, not dark brown. Add soaked cashews for 2 minutes.

3. **Blend.** Cool the onion-cashew mixture before using a sealed bullet blender. Blend with ¾ cup chicken broth until completely smooth.

4. **Bloom spices.** Melt 1 Tbsp unsalted butter on medium-low heat. Bloom 4 cracked cardamom pods, 1-inch cinnamon, 3 cloves and 1 bay leaf for 30–45 seconds. Add 5 minced garlic cloves, 1 Tbsp ginger and the slit deseeded serrano; cook 1 minute. Add 1½ tsp coriander, ½ tsp cumin, ¼ tsp white pepper and a tiny pinch nutmeg; stir 20–30 seconds with a splash of the remaining broth.

5. **Simmer gravy.** Add the onion-cashew purée and stir 3–4 minutes. Gradually stir in the rest of the second ¾ cup broth (1½ cups total). Simmer gently 10–12 minutes, stirring often. Thin with water, up to ½ cup, if needed.

6. **Silky finish.** Remove cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, bay and serrano. Strain for a silky texture if desired. On low heat or off heat stir in ½ cup heavy cream, remaining 1 Tbsp butter, ½ tsp garam masala, ½ tsp crushed kasuri methi and 1 tsp sugar. Taste first; add up to ¼ tsp fine sea salt to start only if needed. Adjust sweetness or heat to taste; a few drops of lemon off heat can lift the richness. Keep warm without boiling.

7. **Mix koftas.** Combine 1½ cups grated paneer, ½ cup cooled grated potato, 2½ Tbsp cornstarch, ½ tsp salt, ½–¾ tsp garam masala or ¼ tsp cardamom, optional 1 Tbsp each chopped cashews and raisins, 1 tsp ginger, 1–2 chopped deseeded green chiles or ½ tsp black pepper, and 1 Tbsp coriander leaves. Mix 2–3 minutes into a smooth nonsticky dough.

8. **Shape and test.** Roll into 12–13 smooth balls. If sticky, lightly dust with cornstarch. Heat neutral oil to 350°F, using enough for deep frying. Test one small kofta; if it breaks, mix in up to 1 Tbsp additional cornstarch and test again.

9. **Fry.** Fry in small batches, undisturbed for the first 2 minutes, until crisp and golden. Lift to a rack or steel colander; allow oil to return to 350°F between batches.

10. **Serve.** Spoon warm gravy into a serving dish. Nestle in the freshly fried koftas just before service; do not simmer them in the gravy. Finish with a little cream, toasted cashew slivers and optional coriander leaves. Serve with Palak Paneer, jeera rice and skillet naan.

## Final Taste Target

Savory first, then rich cashew cream, gentle onion sweetness, cardamom and a clean lingering spice. Pale beige gravy should coat a spoon, while crisp golden koftas stay tender inside.

## Troubleshooting / Rescue Notes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too salty or thick | Thin with water; do not add more regular broth. |
| Too mild | Adjust white pepper, garam masala or a little green chile. |
| Too rich or flat | Taste for salt, then add a few drops of lemon off heat. |
| Kofta breaks | Add a little cornstarch and test again. |

## Chef's Notes

For a restaurant-silky gravy, blend thoroughly and strain before adding cream. Keep the warm gravy loose enough to pool around the koftas.

The gravy can be made ahead and reheated gently; fry koftas just before serving. Serve alongside Palak Paneer for savory green contrast.
