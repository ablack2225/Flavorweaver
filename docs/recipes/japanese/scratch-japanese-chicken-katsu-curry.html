Scratch Japanese Chicken Katsu Curry

Shatter-crisp panko chicken with plain rice, silky dark Japanese curry made from deeply browned onion and scratch butter-flour curry roux, ice-crisp sesame-vinegar cabbage, and a spicy apple-tonkatsu sauce simmered from fruit, vegetables, tomato, Worcestershire, soy, vinegar, chile, and umami.

Kitchen Execution Summary

Protect the Chicken

One large breast is filleted and pounded to an even 1/4 inch, neutral-brined 8-12 hours, dried thoroughly, kept cold, and fried only after the rest of the plate is ready.

Build the Curry Deep

Give the onion 25-35 minutes to turn deeply golden before adding carrot, garlic, ginger, tomato paste, unsweetened applesauce, broth, soy, Worcestershire, honey, MSG, scratch roux, chocolate, and vinegar. Taste the finished curry and use curry powder plus garam masala to taste if it still needs more house-level warm-spice depth.

Keep the Sauces Distinct

The curry is silky, savory, and warm-spiced. The tonkatsu sauce is sharper, fruitier, tangier, and hotter. It is an accent, not a second blanket of sauce.

Fry Last

Rice, curry, tonkatsu sauce, and cold cabbage are ready before breading begins. Dress the cabbage only at service. Katsu goes from 345-350°F oil to wire rack to knife to plate with no holding phase.

Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

8-12 hours before cooking — Dissolve 5 g fine salt + 1 tsp sugar + 1/4 tsp MSG in 2 cups cold water. Submerge 1 large chicken breast already filleted and pounded to 1/4 inch, cover, and refrigerate. If 2 cups does not cover, make more brine using the same ratio. No acid and no baking soda.

30-60 minutes before breading — Remove chicken from brine, do not rinse, pat completely dry, and refrigerate uncovered on a rack or plate.

Make-ahead option — Curry may be made 1 day ahead and reheated gently. Tonkatsu sauce may also be made ahead. Katsu must be fried fresh.

Cabbage prep — The cabbage may be sliced and ice-crisped ahead, but keep it cold and dry. Toss with the sesame-vinegar dressing only immediately before plating.

Service Countdown

75 minutes before serving: remove chicken from brine, dry it, refrigerate uncovered, and start the curry onion.

55 minutes before serving: start rice and finish the curry aromatic base.

40 minutes before serving: blend/strain curry, make the scratch roux, then make the spicy apple-tonkatsu sauce.

20 minutes before serving: shred and ice-crisp cabbage, measure its sesame-vinegar dressing, and stage flour, egg, panko, fryer, rack, thermometer, and knife.

10 minutes before serving: bread chicken and stabilize frying oil at 345-350°F.

At service: finish-season curry to taste, toss the cabbage lightly with dressing, fry, rack-rest 3 minutes, slice, and plate immediately with most of the panko crust exposed.

Reference section only. Once prep bowls are built, cook entirely from the stage bowls and timeline below.

Scratch Curry Roux & Finish

2 Tbsp butter; 2 Tbsp flour; 1 1/2 tsp coriander; 3/4 tsp cumin; 3/4 tsp turmeric; 1/4 tsp fenugreek; 1/4 tsp sweet paprika; 1/4 tsp black pepper; 1/4 tsp cayenne; 1/8 tsp cinnamon; pinch cardamom; tiny pinch clove; tiny pinch nutmeg; 1 tsp chopped 60% dark chocolate; 1 tsp rice vinegar; curry powder to taste; garam masala to taste.

Spicy Apple-Tonkatsu Sauce

1 tsp neutral oil; 2 Tbsp 1/8-inch diced onion; 2 Tbsp finely grated carrot; 3 Tbsp unsweetened applesauce; 1 Tbsp tomato paste; 3 Tbsp water; 1 1/2 Tbsp Worcestershire; 2 tsp soy; 1 tsp oyster sauce; 1 Tbsp rice vinegar; 2 tsp dark brown sugar; 1 tsp chili garlic sauce; 1/4 tsp mustard powder; small pinch MSG.

Katsu Breading

All dried brined chicken; 1/2 cup flour + 1/4 tsp black pepper; 1 large egg + 1 Tbsp cold water; 1 1/4 cups panko; neutral frying oil.

Rice & Sesame-Vinegar Cabbage

1 cup Japanese short-grain rice, rinsed and cooked to rice-cooker directions. If using basmati: 1 cup basmati + 1 1/2 cups water. Plus 2 packed cups cabbage sliced near 1/16 inch / hair-thin, ice water to cover, 1 1/2 tsp rice vinegar, 1 tsp soy, 3/4 tsp toasted sesame oil, 1/2 tsp honey or sugar, small pinch MSG, and 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds.

Do Not Forget

The chicken is already brined: do not salt it again.

Give the curry onion the full 25-35 minutes. Pale onion makes a shallow curry.

Cook tomato paste until brick-red. Do not leave a raw metallic tomato-paste edge.

After curry spices enter the roux, bloom only 30-45 seconds before tempering with hot curry liquid.

Blend and strain the curry if possible for restaurant-level silk.

After chocolate and vinegar are in, taste the finished curry. If it still reads too mild, add curry powder and garam masala to taste in small additions, simmering 30-60 seconds after each addition before tasting again.

Keep the cabbage cold and dry until the last moment. Dress it lightly only at service so it stays crisp rather than becoming slaw.

The tonkatsu sauce is an accent, not a blanket. Keep most panko dry.

Bread only shortly before frying; fry at 345-350°F; rack-rest, never paper-towel steam.

Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

  1. Brine the chicken overnight. Dissolve 5 g fine salt, 1 tsp sugar, and 1/4 tsp MSG in 2 cups cold water. Submerge 1 large breast already filleted and pounded to 1/4 inch, cover, and refrigerate 8-12 hours. No acid and no baking soda.
  2. Dry the chicken and start the curry onion. About 75 minutes before service, remove chicken from brine, do not rinse, pat completely dry, and refrigerate uncovered. Heat 1 Tbsp neutral oil + 1 Tbsp butter over medium-low, add 1 large onion sliced 1/8 inch pole-to-pole, and cook 25-35 minutes until deeply golden-brown, sticky, and sweet-smelling.
  3. Build the curry aromatics. Add 1/2 cup 1/4-inch diced carrot + 2 grated garlic cloves + 2 tsp grated ginger; cook 2-3 minutes. Add 1 Tbsp tomato paste + 1/4 cup unsweetened applesauce; cook another 2-3 minutes until the tomato paste is brick-red and the applesauce has concentrated.
  4. Deglaze and simmer. Add 2 cups chicken broth + 1 Tbsp soy + 2 tsp Worcestershire + 1 tsp honey + 1/2 tsp MSG. Scrape the pan clean and simmer uncovered 15 minutes, until carrot is completely tender.
  5. Start the rice. Cook 1 cup rinsed Japanese short-grain rice to rice-cooker directions. If using basmati, cook 1 cup rinsed basmati + 1 1/2 cups water. Keep plain and warm.
  6. Blend the curry base. Blend the simmered curry completely smooth. Pass through a fine-mesh strainer into a clean saucepan if possible. Keep over very low heat.
  7. Make the scratch roux. Melt 2 Tbsp butter over medium-low. Whisk in 2 Tbsp flour and cook 4-6 minutes until nutty and light peanut-butter colored. Add 1 1/2 tsp coriander, 3/4 tsp cumin, 3/4 tsp turmeric, 1/4 tsp fenugreek, 1/4 tsp sweet paprika, 1/4 tsp black pepper, 1/4 tsp cayenne, 1/8 tsp cinnamon, pinch cardamom, tiny pinch clove, and tiny pinch nutmeg. Cook only 30-45 seconds.
  8. Temper and finish the curry to taste. Gradually whisk about 1/2 cup hot blended curry into the roux until smooth, then whisk that mixture into the main curry. Simmer low 8-10 minutes, stirring frequently. Add 1 tsp chopped 60% dark chocolate during the final 2 minutes. If too thick, loosen with broth or water 1 Tbsp at a time. Stir in 1 tsp rice vinegar at the end. Taste the completed curry; if it is still too mild for the desired spice level, add curry powder and garam masala to taste in small additions, simmering 30-60 seconds after each addition before tasting again.
  9. Make the spicy apple-tonkatsu sauce. Heat 1 tsp oil over medium-low. Add 2 Tbsp 1/8-inch diced onion + 2 Tbsp finely grated carrot + 3 Tbsp unsweetened applesauce; cook 3 minutes. Add 1 Tbsp tomato paste and cook 1-2 minutes. Add 3 Tbsp water + 1 1/2 Tbsp Worcestershire + 2 tsp soy + 1 tsp oyster sauce + 1 Tbsp rice vinegar + 2 tsp dark brown sugar + 1 tsp chili garlic sauce + 1/4 tsp mustard powder + pinch MSG. Simmer 7-10 minutes, blend smooth, and loosen only if necessary with water 1 tsp at a time.
  10. Ice-crisp and lightly dress the cabbage. Submerge 2 packed cups hair-thin cabbage in ice water for 10 minutes. Drain and dry thoroughly, then keep cold. Combine 1 1/2 tsp rice vinegar + 1 tsp soy + 3/4 tsp toasted sesame oil + 1/2 tsp honey or sugar + small pinch MSG + 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds. Immediately before plating, toss the cabbage lightly with the dressing so it stays feathery and crisp.
  11. Build the breading line. Set out 1/2 cup flour + 1/4 tsp black pepper, 1 egg + 1 Tbsp cold water beaten smooth, and 1 1/4 cups panko. Remove the cold dried chicken and divide into 2 even cutlets if one large piece is awkward. Do not salt again.
  12. Bread once, cleanly. Flour each cutlet completely and shake off excess. Coat fully in egg wash. Press gently into panko so the flakes adhere without being crushed. Rest on a rack 5 minutes while the oil stabilizes.
  13. Fry the katsu last. Heat neutral oil to 345-350°F. Fry without crowding until deeply golden and crisp, about 3-4 minutes total for 1/4-inch breast, turning only if needed. Cook to 165°F in the center. Transfer to a wire rack and rest 3 minutes.
  14. Slice decisively. Use a sharp knife and straight downward cuts to slice each cutlet into 3/4-inch strips. Do not saw through the crust.
  15. Serve the full plate. Spoon plain warm rice to one side of each plate, pour curry beside it with only a little lapping onto the rice, add a compact pile of cold lightly dressed sesame-vinegar cabbage, then place sliced katsu so most of the crust remains exposed. Add spicy apple-tonkatsu sauce as a narrow stripe over only part of the cutlet or serve it in a ramekin for dipping. Serve immediately.

Final Taste Target

The plate should announce crisp chicken and dark savory Japanese curry first. The curry carries deeply browned onion, warm curry spices, garlic, ginger, tomato, restrained apple, soy-Worcestershire depth, and a tiny roasted chocolate bass note with rice vinegar lifting the finish; final curry powder and garam masala are adjusted to taste so the curry never lands muted. The katsu must remain moist inside and dry-crisp outside. The spicy apple-tonkatsu sauce is brighter and sharper than the curry, with fruit, tomato, vinegar, Worcestershire, mustard, chile, and umami. Plain rice gives the curry room to lead, while the cold sesame-vinegar cabbage supplies crisp, lightly savory-acidic relief instead of a flavorless reset. Nothing should taste boxed, ketchup-heavy, greasy, or muted.

Chef's Notes

The onions earn the curry. If they are pale, keep cooking. Flavorweaver boldness here comes from caramelization, spice bloom, umami, acid, chile, and final warm-spice adjustment—not simply adding more sugar.

Unsweetened applesauce is intentional. It integrates quickly into the curry and tonkatsu sauce; cook it with tomato so the plate stays savory-first.

Finish seasoning is intentionally flexible. Once chocolate and vinegar are in, taste. Curry powder and garam masala are there to lift a batch that still tastes too restrained, not to overpower the scratch curry base.

The cabbage has to earn its place. Keep the traditional hair-thin cold crunch, but use the light sesame-vinegar dressing so it tastes like a component rather than decoration.

Protect the crust. Curry and tonkatsu sauce should create sauced bites and dry crunchy bites on the same plate.

Protect the breast. The 1/4-inch geometry and overnight brine let the crust brown quickly. Do not extend frying just to deepen color once the center is cooked.
