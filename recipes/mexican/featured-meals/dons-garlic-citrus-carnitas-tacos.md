# Don's Garlic-Citrus Carnitas Tacos

> Garlic-citrus pork butt cooked slowly until succulent in its own rendered fat, broken into substantial pieces, hard-crisped with concentrated pork jus, and served in warm corn tortillas with charred serrano-chipotle salsa, white onion, cilantro, and lime.

## Before Cooking Day / Advance Prep

- **24 hours before cooking** — Coat the pork with the measured garlic-citrus aromatic marinade and refrigerate tightly covered.
- **Orange handling** — Orange marmalade replaces fresh orange zest in the marinade; orange juice is reserved for the slow cook.
- **Pork fat** — Keep visible exterior and seam fat attached. The pork butt must self-render enough fat for crisping.
- **Equipment check** — Use a heavy Dutch oven that holds the pork in a snug single layer, plus a blender, small saucepan, and large electric skillet.
- **Service ingredients** — Confirm corn tortillas, white onion, cilantro, limes, tomatoes, serranos, jalapeno, and chipotle in adobo before starting the cook.

## Do Not Forget

- Keep the pork butt's visible fat attached; it is the source of the cooking and crisping fat.
- Do not add orange juice to the 24-hour marinade.
- Do not rinse or scrape the aromatic marinade from the pork before cooking.
- Use a snug pot and pour the liquid around the pork rather than over its seasoned surfaces.
- Tenderness is the deciding doneness test; do not stop at a number if the pork still resists a skewer.
- Keep the pork in substantial irregular pieces rather than finely shredding it.
- Separate rendered fat from pork juices before reducing the jus.
- Use only enough reduced jus to season and caramelize the crisped pork.
- Taste the cooked salsa before using a Thai chile for extra heat.
- Crisp the pork and warm tortillas close to service.
- Taste before plating and adjust salt, acid, heat, texture, and final freshness.

## Full Cooking Timeline and Instructions

1. **Marinate the pork 24 hours ahead.** Combine **10 g kosher salt**, **6 finely grated garlic cloves**, **1 1/2 tsp orange marmalade**, **1 tsp lime zest**, **2 tsp crushed Mexican oregano**, **1 tsp black pepper**, **3/4 tsp ground cumin**, **1/8 tsp ground cinnamon**, and **1 Tbsp neutral oil**. Massage the paste over all surfaces of **2.25 lb pork butt cut into 2- to 3-inch cubes**. Cover tightly and refrigerate for **24 hours**. Do not add orange juice to this long marinade.

2. **Temper the pork and heat the oven.** Remove the marinated pork from the refrigerator **30 minutes** before cooking. Heat the oven to **300°F**. Do not rinse or scrape away the marinade.

3. **Stage the self-rendering cook.** Arrange the marinated pork snugly in a heavy Dutch oven, placing fattier surfaces against the bottom. Tuck **1/2 medium white onion in thick wedges**, **3 lightly crushed garlic cloves**, **1 bay leaf**, and **1/2 tsp Mexican oregano** around the pork.

4. **Add the citrus cooking liquid.** Combine **1/2 cup orange juice**, **3/4 cup water**, and **1 Tbsp fresh lime juice**. Pour the mixture around the pork instead of directly over the seasoned surfaces. Bring just to a gentle simmer on the stovetop.

5. **Cook covered.** Cover the Dutch oven and transfer it to the **300°F oven**. Cook for **1 hour 45 minutes** without aggressively boiling the pork.

6. **Uncover, render, and tenderize.** Remove the lid and gently turn the pieces. Continue cooking uncovered for **45 to 75 minutes**, turning approximately every **20 minutes**. If the pot becomes dry before the pork is tender, add **1/4 cup hot water**. Remove the pork when a skewer enters with almost no resistance and the meat separates under gentle pressure while still holding substantial pieces. The internal temperature will usually be around **195 to 205°F**, but tenderness is the deciding test.

7. **Char the salsa vegetables.** While the pork finishes, char **2 Roma tomatoes**, **2 whole serranos**, **1 whole jalapeno**, **1/4 medium white onion**, and **3 unpeeled garlic cloves** beneath a broiler or in a dry skillet. Turn as needed until the vegetables are blistered and softened. Peel the garlic and remove the chile stems; retain the chile seeds for strong heat.

8. **Blend the salsa.** Blend the charred vegetables with **1 chipotle in adobo**, **1 tsp adobo sauce**, **1 Tbsp apple-cider vinegar**, **1/2 tsp kosher salt**, and **2 Tbsp water** until cohesive but slightly textured. Add up to **2 Tbsp more water** only if the blender needs it.

9. **Fry and finish the salsa.** Heat **1 Tbsp neutral oil** in a saucepan. Carefully add the blended salsa and cook for **5 to 8 minutes**, stirring, until darker, glossy, and concentrated. Finish with **1 tsp lime juice**; add the second teaspoon only if needed. Taste the cooked salsa before deciding whether to blend in **1/2 fresh Thai chile** for more heat. If added, simmer another **2 minutes** and reassess before using more.

10. **Rest the pork and separate the pot liquid.** Transfer the tender pork to a tray and rest for **10 to 15 minutes**. Strain the Dutch-oven liquid into a heatproof container; discard the spent onion, garlic, and bay leaf. Let the liquid settle, then spoon the clear rendered pork fat into a separate bowl.

11. **Reduce the pork-citrus jus.** Pour the defatted pork juices into a small saucepan and simmer until reduced to approximately **1/4 cup**. Taste before seasoning. Add **1/2 to 1 tsp lime juice** only if it needs a fresher edge. Do not automatically add salt because the jus concentrates the pork's original seasoning.

12. **Prepare the final crisp.** Break the rested pork into irregular **1- to 1 1/2-inch pieces**, preserving natural edges and fatty seams. Do not finely shred it. Heat the large electric skillet to approximately **300°F** and test one piece because the skillet runs hot.

13. **Hard-crisp the carnitas.** Add **2 Tbsp reserved rendered pork fat** to the skillet. Arrange the pork in one uncrowded layer and cook undisturbed for **3 to 4 minutes**, until the first surface develops a dark substantial crust. Turn and crisp another side for **2 to 3 minutes**.

14. **Caramelize with controlled jus.** Drizzle **2 Tbsp reduced pork-citrus jus around the pork**, not over every crisp surface. Toss once and cook **30 to 60 seconds**, until the jus evaporates and caramelizes. Taste one piece. Add no more than **1 additional Tbsp jus** if the meat needs greater intensity.

15. **Warm the tortillas.** Lightly brush **10 to 12 corn tortillas** with additional reserved pork fat if available. Warm for **20 to 30 seconds per side** until pliable with scattered toasted spots. If little fat rendered, warm the tortillas dry rather than substituting beef fat. Hold beneath a clean towel.

16. **Build and serve.** Fill each warm tortilla with a generous portion of crisp carnitas. Add charred serrano-chipotle salsa, finely diced white onion, chopped cilantro, and a squeeze of fresh lime. Serve immediately with extra salsa and lime at the table.

## Building the Final Tacos

| Layer | Build Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Warm Tortilla | Use one warm corn tortilla per taco; double only if the tortilla is fragile. |
| 2. Carnitas Anchor | Add a generous portion of hot pork with both juicy interior meat and dark crisp edges. |
| 3. Salsa | Spoon on enough charred serrano-chipotle salsa to cut the pork fat without drowning the crust. |
| 4. Raw Aromatic Crunch | Add finely diced white onion and chopped cilantro. |
| 5. Acid Finish | Squeeze fresh lime over the assembled taco immediately before eating. |
| 6. Table Service | Keep extra salsa and lime available; do not pre-sauce tacos that will sit. |

## Final Taste Target

The pork should taste unmistakably like pork first, followed by roasted garlic, clear orange-lime lift, Mexican oregano, recognizable cumin, and a quiet cinnamon undertone.

Each taco should move from succulent interior pork to dark crisp edges, concentrated savory citrus jus, charred fresh-chile heat, restrained chipotle smoke, raw onion crunch, cilantro, and clean lime. The salsa must be forceful enough to cut the fat, but the pork must remain the center of the bite.

The dish should not taste like sweet pulled pork, wet braised pork, generic taco seasoning, canned fruit, or a chipotle-dominated adobo sauce.

## Chef's Notes

The water and citrus liquid protect the pork during the first phase while its own fat renders. Once uncovered, the remaining liquid reduces and the pork begins behaving more like small-batch carnitas cooked in freshly rendered lard.

Orange marmalade is used in a very small measured amount because it supplies peel character when fresh orange zest is unavailable. More marmalade would push the pork toward sweetness and interfere with clean crisping.

The reduced pork-citrus jus is a seasoning tool, not a finishing sauce. Add it around already-crisp pork so it can evaporate and caramelize without soaking the crust.

For leftovers, store tender pork, rendered fat, and jus separately when practical. Crisp only the portion being served.

## Source / Research Notes

- Traditional carnitas technique protects pork identity through slow fat-assisted cooking and a vigorous final crisp.
- The small-batch self-rendering method uses water for early protection, then relies on the pork butt's own rendered fat after evaporation.
- Chef-level versions reviewed emphasized substantial pork pieces, salt penetration, rendered-fat cooking, concentrated pork flavor, and final crisping near service.
- Rick Bayless carnitas and crisped-meat techniques informed the pork-forward restraint and late hard crisp.
- Jonathan Zaragoza's carnitas work informed the overnight seasoning and slow lard-based texture target.
- The Amy & Don house variation deliberately restores Don's requested garlic, citrus, cumin, and cinnamon identity in controlled layers.
- Fresh serrano and jalapeno plus one chipotle in adobo were selected around the actual pantry; Thai chile remains an adjustment tool rather than the salsa's core identity.
- Orange marmalade and orange juice replace fresh orange zest and fruit for this first test; canned mandarins are intentionally excluded.
